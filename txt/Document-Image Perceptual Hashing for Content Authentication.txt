Document-Image Perceptual Hashing for

Xiaotong Situ, Tong Liu, Heng Yao , Member, IEEE, Chuan Qin , Member, IEEE,
and Xinpeng Zhang , Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract—This paper proposes an end-to-end two-branch network for a document image perceptual hashing scheme, where the
two branches focus on image visual features and text features,
respectively. Existing perceptual hashing schemes cannot solve
the problem of the tiny proportion of text tampering detection,
while simple text detection is unable to solve the problem of
background region-aware matching. To address these issues, we
extract text information via optical character recognition (OCR)
and then generate the text features using the bidirectional encoder
representations from Transformers (BERT). Visual features of the
image are extracted from the local and global features of the image
using ResNet and Vision Transformer cascades, and then fused
to generate the final hash sequence through the fully connected
layer. The proposed network considers both image visual features
and textual information to verify that the document image has not
been tampered with. In our network, the OCR module enables
accurate and intelligent text detection and recognition, particularly
for dealing with text tampering that has only been conducted in tiny
portions. It also provides more efficient and robust text recognition
services. Experimental results show that the proposed hashing
scheme is robust and discriminative in document images.
Index Terms—Perceptual image hashing, document image, tam
pering detection, two-branch network.

II. RELATED WORK
A. Document Image Editing and Tampering
With deep learning, image editing algorithms can tamper with
the textual information of an image in an end-to-end manner,
thus posing a considerable security threat to document images.
Wu et al. [3] introduced a network designed for text image
editing that can replace text in scene text without destroying the
image’s inherent stylistic features. The technique first learns the
style of foreground text and then transfers this style to new text

The squeezing-excitation (SE) network proposed by Zhang et

streams, which utilizes image edges to learn the splicing mask.
These advanced text editing techniques highlight the growing
need for robust document image authentication methods.
B. Perceptual Image Hashing
According to the different feature extraction of image hashing, the traditional hashing schemes can be categorized into
three types. Based on the spatial domain approach, Huang and
Liu [15] extracted the maximum gradient and the corresponding
orientation information from RGB color channels, and then
further obtained the normalized histogram and local frequency
coefficient features to generate the hash sequences. Su et al. [16]
proposed a new personalized authentication framework, which
incorporates the distance metric learning technique and the
supervised quantization strategy into the image authentication
process. Based on the transformed domain methods, they mainly
include orthogonal and integral transforms to extract image
features in the transform domain to generate hash. In [17],
the image hash was generated based on the discrete cosine
transform invariance at the same locations in separate blocks
of the image. Tang et al. [18] proposed to present the edge
image of the normalized image by Canny’s algorithm and applied the 2D-discret wavelet transform (2D-DWT) to obtain
the different sub-bands and calculate the weighted sum of the
DWT of these sub-bands for generating the hash. Dimensionality
reduction methods mainly utilize data dimensionality reduction
algorithms to construct image hashes through low dimensional
feature data, such as singular value decomposition [19], local
linear embedding, nonnegative matrix factorization [20], and
principal component analysis (PCA). Tang et al. [21] utilized
quaternionic singular value decomposition to construct image
hashes, improving the algorithm’s uniqueness.
With the advancement of image processing technology and
the development of deep learning, deep neural networks have
been increasingly used in image hashing algorithms and have
achieved better performance than traditional algorithms. Li et
al. [22] applied deep learning methods to the field of perceptual
hashing and demonstrated performance that outperformed traditional hashing algorithms. They proposed a robust and secure
image hashing scheme based on deep neural networks, which
utilized denoising self-coding to train to restore distorted images
to their original state. Qin et al. [10] proposed a perceptual
hashing algorithm based on a multi-constrained convolutional
neural network using two constraints to model training and
dynamically adjust the constraint values to improve perceptual
robustness and discriminative ability. Liu et al. [23] proposed
a Swin Transformer-based perceptual image hashing scheme to

resist the effect of LCD screen distortion on content authentication; the scheme added a distortion simulator to assist the
feature extractor and hash generator to learn and resist screen
distortion. Yu et al. [24] proposed a robust hashing scheme with
deep features and Meixner moments for image copy detection,
extracting global deep features and local features separately.
Hash is ultimately determined by quantifying and combining
global depth features and local features.
The development of robust perceptual hashing methods for
document images based on deep learning hashing technology
provides a solution. The traditional method is based on artificial
features, by comparing the histogram, edge features, and texture
features of images to evaluate the similarity of documents. It is
difficult to fully and accurately describe a document image’s semantic and content characteristics because of the characteristics
of manual design. The deep learning model can automatically
learn the complex features in document images. Our work integrates visual and textual features to achieve comprehensive
tampering detection and content authentication.
C. Perceptual Image Hashing versus Semantic Retrieval
Hashing
Image hashing research primarily focuses on semantic image
hashing retrieval and perceptual hashing authentication. Although both rely on hashing techniques, their goals and methods
are somewhat different. Semantic retrieval hashing aims to find
images semantically similar to the query image. For instance,
Xing et al. [25] proposed deep incremental hashing, which
uses a pointwise loss function to guide hash neural network
learning and combines hash tables from different time steps
via a weighted method to form a multi-table hash system.
Nie et al. [26] introduced ssupervised discrete multiple-length
hashin, enabling simultaneous learning of hash codes with multiple lengths, significantly improving retrieval flexibility and
accuracy. Yang et al. [27] developed robust discrete hashing
for large-scale image semantic indexing, which learns discrete
binary codes and hash functions in a unified model. Lu et
al. [28] proposed a subregion-guided transformer hash network
for fine-grained image retrieval, generating compact binary
codes through partial effective region erasure and multi-stage
hybrid loss. Cao et al. [29] designed a privacy-preserving image
retrieval framework using cycle-consistent adversarial networks
and visual graph convolutional network to enhance retrieval
accuracy. While semantic image hashing methods perform well
in capturing high-level semantic information for efficient image
retrieval, they are not directly applicable to document image
authentication tasks.
D. Bidirectional Encoder Representations From Transformers

information. BERT is a pre-trained language representation
model proposed by Google [30] in 2018, which has shown great
success in a wide range. The core of the BERT model is based on
the Transformer architecture, which is capable of understanding
and generating complex nuances and contextual dependencies in
text through deep bi-directional learning. The pre-training of the

III. PROPOSED METHOD
This section describes our proposed framework in detail. First,
Section III-A introduces the overall architecture of the network,
which consists of three main stages: text feature extractor, image
feature extractor, feature fusion, and loss function detailed in
Section III-B, III-C, III-D and III-E, respectively.
A. Overall Architecture
In this subsection, we present the ResViT-OCR hashing
framework. Fig. 3 illustrates the proposed overall framework,
which consists of a text-modal branch and an image-model
branch, each including feature learning modules. The image and
text are converted to a common coding space where similarities
between image and text are preserved. The text features are
converted to the feature size required for image modality using
a fully connected (FC) layer, and then fused together. Finally, a
hash sequence of length 50 is generated by an FC layer.
Specifically, we preprocess the training data to unify the image
size and then feed it into the image and text feature extractors.
The OCR module extracts the text content, and the extracted
text information is input into BERT to generate text feature
fT . Similarly, the image is generated by the ResViT module
to generate an image feature fI . Then, fT and fI with the same
size are fused to form the text image feature map. The hash
generator converts the multidimensional feature map into a
fixed-length image hash sequence. The effective combination
of feature extractor and hash generator realizes the end-to-end
learning process of the network model.
B. Text Extraction Module
The OCR module consists of two main parts: text region
detection based on the Connectionist Text Proposal Network

(CTPN) [31] and text recognition based on the Convolutional
Recurrent Neural Network (CRNN) [32]. The CTPN combines the advantages of CNNs and Recurrent Neural Networks
(RNNs) to generate high-quality text candidate regions. It further
utilizes a regression network to pinpoint text box locations. The
network architecture is shown in Fig. 4. Feature maps are extracted from the input image using CNNs, typically a pre-trained
CNN (e.g. VGG or ResNet). The size of the feature map is
C × H × W , where H and W denote the height and width of the
image, and C is the number of channels of feature maps. On the
last convolutional layer (Conv5) of the CNN, a spatial window
of 3 × 3 size is used for dense sliding to generate the learned
spatial features. These features are utilized to make predictions
for multiple anchors, where the convolutional features of each
window are 3 × 3 × C. The obtained features are then input
into a 256-dimensional bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory
(BLSTM) Network, which captures the visual information of
the image and provides the basis for subsequent text candidate
generation. The output of the BLSTM network is input into
the FC layer, and designs K vertical anchors to predict the
y-coordinate of each proposal. The FC layer outputs three parts:
2K vertical coordinates represent the height of the selection box
and the y-axis coordinates of the center; 2K scores represent the
category information of the K anchors, stating whether they are
characters or not; and K side-refinement offsets represent the
horizontal offsets of the selection boxes. Then adjust the predicted anchors to determine the text candidate regions. Finally,
output the accurate localized position of the text.

into several non-overlapping patches of a fixed size S × S by
ViT. These patches are then linearly expanded and converted into
a series of tokens through a FC layer, with positional encoding
added to preserve their spatial information. The transformed
tokens are then fed into ViT’s encoder for further processing.
Inside the encoder, the self-attention mechanism allows the
model to consider the information of all other tokens while
processing each token. The three matrices Q, K, and V are
obtained by multiplying the tokens with three learnable matrices.

the similarity between tokens, generating an attention scores
matrix. A Softmax function processes this matrix to obtain a
probability distribution, which is then multiplied with V to
produce a weighted output. The model captures relationships
between tokens from multiple perspectives by repeatedly applying the multi-head attention mechanism. The tokens, after
being processed through multiple encoder layers, are eventually

D. Feature Fusion
Before feature fusion, we use BERT to extract the textual
feature of the sentence. We have chosen the only Chinese
BERT model published by Google [33], because our training
set and test set are Chinese document images. The BERT model
is replaceable, and if we want to process English document
images, we can choose other BERT models. BERT efficiently
represents highly unstructured textual data as vectors, which is
a trained stack of Transformer encoders. After extracting the
textual information, we embed some special tags to distinguish
each different sentence. At the beginning of the sentence, we
embed ’[CLS]’ as the first token of each sequence, refer to the
sequence of input tokens passed to BERT, and embed ’[SEP]’
at the end of the sentence. We can distinguish sentences by
this unique token. Once the input tokens are ready, they are
processed by each layer, which applies self-attention, passes the
result through a feed-forward network, and hands it off to the
next encoder. In terms of architecture, it remains the same as
the Transformer. After extracting the text feature, it is fed into a
FC layer, which means that the text and image features have the
exact dimensions. Finally, the two features are spliced and fed
into a FC layer to generate the final hash sequence.

IV. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
In this section, we first describe the datasets and training
parameter settings. In order to evaluate the effectiveness of the
proposed model, we conduct a series of experiments, including robustness, discriminative ability, discussion of threshold,
authentication ability, adversarial attacks, ablation experiments,
computational complexity, and key-dependent performance.
A. Experiment Setup
1) Dataset Construction: We construct a text tampering
dataset to evaluate the proposed scheme’s effectiveness and
superiority. We employ the tampering method from [34], which
selectively tampers to generate realistic images of tampered documents. In [34], it consists of selective copy-paste and selective
generation. The former acquires groups of texts with similar
styles and performs copy-paste in the grouped text instances to
generate the tampered texts. The latter uses OpenCV [35] to
erase their original text content and then prints the new text with
a predefined similar style and font. Following [34], the entire
data synthesis approach for text tampering we used is described
as follows: (1) use powerful open-source OCR tools such as
PPOCRLabel in Paddle-OCR [36] to obtain the bounding boxes
of the words and characters and extract the parts of the text
that we want to tamper; (2) use styleText [3] to separate the
foreground color of the text from its background and use the
background to generate new text content; (3) selectively copy

and paste the generated new text content to the original image
to obtain the tampered document image.
We randomly select 500 document images as original images
from the Document Forgery Attack dataset of Alibaba Tenchi
Competition [37]. Using the method described above, we tamper
with these images so that each original image yields 35 corresponding tampered versions. Based on this, we perform multiple
content-preserving operations with different parameters on each
image, as listed in Table II. We use seven types of image
content-preserving operations, including JPEG compression,
Gaussian noise, cropping, and scaling. These adjustments to the
document images do not affect the semantic information they
contain; hence, we all consider the images to be perceptually
similar. Therefore, each original image has 35 perceptually
similar versions, and the proposed document image perceptual
hashing dataset for training, referred to as DIPHD1, consists of
500 + 500 × 35 + 500 × 35 = 35, 500 images. In order to ensure the diversity of the detection data, we also choose DocuImg,
a document image dataset proposed by Chen et al. [38] as the
detection database for the following experiments.
2) Implementation and Training Details: The framework
proposed in this paper is implemented in Pytorch and trained
on an NVIDIA RTX3090 GPU to obtain the final hash model.
We train the OCR module and the document image perceptual
hashing module in steps. The Synthetic Word dataset [39] is
used during the training of the OCR module, which is widely
used in computer vision and machine learning, especially in
text recognition and detection tasks. This dataset consists of
synthetically generated images, each of which is automatically
generated and contains text in different fonts, sizes, colors, and
backgrounds to cover a wide range of possible scenarios. For
the training of the OCR module, we use RMSProp [40] as the
optimizer and set the learning rate as 1 × 10−4 . The epoch of
the model is set to 200.
In training the document image perceptual hash model, we
use our proposed and constructed tampered document image
dataset DIPHD1. This dataset includes the original document
images, the similar version of the original images, and the
tampered version of the original images. In addition, we set
the training learning rate to e−4 , optimize the algorithm using
the Adam optimizer [41], and the corresponding batch size is
set to 71. Respectively, the model’s epoch is set to obtain the
final perceptual hash network. Before feeding the images into
the model, we normalized the images to a size of 224 × 224 for
model training. In addition, the length of the hash sequence is
set to 50.

V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a perceptual hashing algorithm for
document images. The whole algorithm is divided into OCR
modules and text image hash modules, and they are trained
separately to compose a complete hashing framework. The text
information recognized by OCR is used to detect the tampering
of characters in the document image, while the perceptual image
hash module is used to detect the tampering of visual features
such as background and masking of the image. Combining
the OCR system with perceptual image hashing technology, it
realizes all-around content authentication of document images,
taking into account both textual and visual features of document
images. In addition, by adopting a deep residual network with
ViT cascade, we realize the comprehensive extraction of visual
features of the image. In order to verify the effectiveness of the
proposed algorithm, we have designed a series of experiments.
The experimental results show that the proposed perceptual
hashing scheme exhibits excellent performance in the face of
tampering detection of document images. Experimental comparisons with other state-of-the-art schemes and results based on
ROC and F1 score evaluation metrics show that our algorithm
also achieves satisfactory results in terms of robustness and
discrimination.
We will continue improving tamper detection performance
under OCR noise in future research. In addition, we will further
improve the proposed scheme by exploring more effective fusion
methods to integrate text and image feature information, simplifying the model complexity by utilizing knowledge distillation
and quantization, and developing more lightweight modules to
enhance computational efficiency.
