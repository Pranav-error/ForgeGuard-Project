Abstract—The process of preserving, identifying, extracting,
and documenting computer evidence that can be utilized in a
court of law is known as digital forensics. It is the science of
finding evidence from digital media like a computer, mobile
phone, server, or network. It equips the forensic team with
the most up-to-date techniques and tools for resolving complex
digital cases.. Among many of them, the major problem which
is addressed in this project is Image Forgery Detection. More
precisely we deal here with the “splicing attack detection and
localization”. Even though there are numerous techniques to
conquer the problem, still there are loopholes in them like the
accuracy of the forged region localization. As a part of this
project, we propose a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)
based Deep Learning architecture, which is capable of detecting
this forgery with state-of-the-art performance. We propose a
transfer learning (EfficientNet) based image classification algorithm for the classification of forged Images and EfficientNet
based U-Net Architecture for localization. In the classification
part, we extract the image patches from the forged regions
and non-forged regions in an image and classify them. The
classification fetched us state of the art result. In the localization
part, we have built a U-Net architecture by replacing the U-Net
Encoder part with EfficientNet layers and keeping the decoder
part as usual. We then pass the forged images through the
network to train the model. The localization experiment also
fetched state-of-the-art results.
Keywords—Digital Forensics; Image Forgery Detection; Splicing Attack; Convolutional Neural Network; Instance Segmentation; U-Net; Transfer Learning;

I. I NTRODUCTION
Recently, we have witnessed advances in user-friendly image editing software, image editing techniques. So image
manipulation has become very easy and affordable [1]. Forgers
may employ forged photos to transmit misleading information,
spread rumours, or give false testimony, all of which have
detrimental social consequences. Image forensics, which aims
to differentiate counterfeit photographs and prevent forgers
from utilising them for unethical business or political reasons,
has sparked a lot of interest in the scientific and industrial
areas. To find the altered regions, various clues are analysed
like the splicing, copy-move, and removal, etc. The image
splicing basically copies regions from an authentic image
and pastes them to other images, copy-move forgery copies
and pastes regions within the same image. It’s really hard to
identify the tampered regions even after careful inspection.

The traditional feature extraction-based methods can be
categorised into four types: detection methods based on the
essence attribute [2] [3] [4], detection methods based on the
imaging device attribute [5] [6] [7] [8], detection methods
based on the image compression attribute [9] [10] [11], and
detection methods based on the hash techniques [12] [13] [14].
However, the traditional features extraction techniques persist
with lots of drawbacks. i) Firstly image essence techniques
fail if some hidden processes are performed after the process
of splicing forgery. ii) In case the device noise intensity of an
image is weak, the detection methods based on the imaging
device fails. iii) The detection based on the image compression
attribute only detects the images saved by JPEG format. iv)
The hash techniques rely on the hash of the original untampered image. So we cannot rely on one single forgery
detection method.
Convolutional neural networks (CNN) have achieved great
success in the research area of computer vision. CNN has been
used by researchers to accomplish splicing forgery detection.
CNN is originally used to judge whether the image has been
forged in [15], but it couldn’t locate the tampered regions. In
[15] the authors tried to locate the tampered regions by CNNs,
but the detected regions showed inaccurate rough areas that are
made up of some square white blocks. To improve researchers
tried to improve the detected tampered regions, the methods
[16] [17] use the non-overlapping image patch as the input of
CNNs. However, when an image patch totally comes from the

two networks, one (C-CNN) to detect forged regions and

II. P ROPOSED M ETHODS
A. Proposed Splicing Detection Model
To improve the classification accuracy and optimum localization of the forged regions, we propose two novel Image
classification and localization networks. In the classification
part, we transformed the images in such a way so that our
algorithm could learn the features more precisely and fetch us
optimum results. For the initial part, we took the idea from this
[15] paper. Every fictitious image has a mask that corresponds
to it. We utilize that mask to sample the forged image along the
spliced region’s boundary in such a way that both the forged
and unforged parts of the image contribute at least 25% of
the total. These samples will have distinct boundaries that are
only present in bogus photographs. The CNN we develop will
have to learn these boundaries. Similarly, we extract a similar
number of patches from the authentic images. We then classify
them using our CNN model. We used transfer learning,
EfficientNet [21] for the classification purpose. EfficientNet is
the recent state-of-the-art ImageNet competition classification
model. We achieved state-of-the-art results after performing
the experiment.
Image classification of this experiment is about classification between forged and authentic images. We have chosen
the CASIA 1 dataset for this experiment. The dataset consists
of authentic images, spliced images, and masks related to the
forged images. Splicing attack forgery detection takes place
when we paste a portion of an image on top of another image.
As we have discussed above, we try to extract image patches
of the boundaries from the forged image which consists of
pixels from the forged portion as well as pixels from an
authentic portion of an image. We identify the boundaries
using the mask of each image. The mask contains a white
portion (255) which is the forged and a black portion (0) which
is authentic. Programmatically we extract boundary image

patches that contain both these types of pixels. Similarly, we
extract a similar number of patches from authentic images.
These boundaries are to be learned by the CNN architecture
that we design.
To make boundaries even more distinct, the gray-scale
images were converted to binary Otsu’s thresholding (implemented in Open CV) after de-noising using the Gaussian filter.
After this operation, sampling was merely a matter of moving
64*64 windows (with a stride of 8) through the fake image
and counting 0 valued(black) pixels in the corresponding mask
and sampling in case the value lies in a certain interval.
B. Proposed Splicing Localisation Model
In the case of the localization part, we use transfer learningenabled U-Net architecture [22]. The U-Net architecture consists of encoding and decoding layers. In the encoding part,
we used layers of EfficientNet and for decoding, we used
the default layers of U-Net. This experiment also fetched us
state-of-the-art results. The inference part of the forged images
was very impressive performing above all the existing forgery
localization algorithms.
We know that Image classification using transfer learning
architectures consists of multiple layers of neural networks.
There is a strong chance of overfitting in such cases. To
avoid the same, there is a requirement for a large dataset.
The CASIA1 dataset contains 462 spliced images which are
solely not sufficient to train the best model. Therefore we have
combined both CASIA1 and CASIA2 datasets containing 462
and 1765 images respectively. Now 2226 spliced and authentic
images individually are good numbers to train our model.

C. Deep Learning Model using Transfer Learning for classification
We leveraged transfer learning using the pre-trained models
on the ImageNet dataset for classification purposes. Initially,
we used the ResNet50 model with ’binary cross-entropy loss
and Adam optimizer having a learning rate of 1e-6. We have
run the model for 15 epochs. It fetched a test accuracy of
94.15% with a loss of 0.31.
To improve further we leveraged the latest state-of-the-art
ImageNet model, EfficientNet. AutoML MNAS created the
EfficientNet-B0 baseline network, and Efficient-B1 to B7 are
acquired by scaling up the baseline network. The architecture
uses the mobile inverted bottleneck convolution (MBConv).
The researchers then scaled up this baseline network to create
the EfficientNets family of deep learning models. Firstly, we
used the EfficientNetB0 as the base architecture with Adam
optimizer (learning rate of 1e-3). It fetched an accuracy of
97.12% with a 0.11 loss. The steps are depicted in Fig. 2.
To further increase the accuracy we leveraged the EfficientNetB2 with Adam optimizer (learning rate of 1e-3). It achieved
an accuracy of 98.26% with a loss of 0.054. The result was
very close to state-of-the-art accuracy. To further improve the
accuracy we leveraged the EfficientNetB3. We use the Adam
optimizer (learning rate 1e-4). Finally, we achieved the state
of the art accuracy of 99.02% with a loss of 0.046.

D. U-Net Architecture using Transfer Learning for Localisation
As discussed above we have used U-Net Architecture
for the localization of the spliced regions. U-Net is a
state-of-the-art Image segmentation algorithm. We have
incorporated some major changes in the architecture to fetch
out optimum accuracy. The U-Net architecture consists of
two parts encoding and decoding. Since the layers of the
architecture can be easily modified or replaced as per our
requirement, we have leveraged transfer learning layers in the
encoding part. The EfficientNet architecture is the modern
state-of-the-art Image classification architecture that fetched
optimum complexity with minimum use of deep networks. We
have changed the encoding part with the EfficientNet layers
with a similar number of neurons in each layer. The decoding
part of the U-Net is left as it is. Apart from the Precision,
recall, and f1-score. We would be using dice-coefficient
here, which is essentially used for semantic segmentation
performance analysis.
We have firstly incorporated the EfficientNetB0 as the
encoding layer of our network. Similarly, we have extracted
few skip connection layers and concatenated them with the
encoder output to perform the upsampling operation. We have
used relu activation function and batch normalization at each
subsequent layer. The architecture is a semantic segmentation
technique that essentially links pixels to the class label. The
class label here is forged and un-forged regions. The regions
and their corresponding pixels are identified by the model
using the ground truth of each spliced image. We can think
of semantic segmentation as image classification at a pixel
level. The performance of the EfficientNetB0 wasn’t up to the
mark. We repeated the same procedure with other EfficientNet Architectures as well. Subsequently, we incorporated the
EfficientNetB7 as our encoding layer and that fetched us the
state-of-the-art result. The model secured a dice coefficient of
0.78, precision of 0.86, recall of 0.74, F1-score of 0.8, and
AUC of 0.87.

IV. C ONCLUSION
In this work, Image Splicing Detection and Localisation
using EfficientNet and Modified U-Net Architecture is proposed for splicing attack forgery detection and localisation.
In classification part we have extracted the image patches
from the forged and authentic regions to distinguish the border
parts better and classify using transfer learning technique
between the forged image and the authentic image. In case
of localisation we have used a modified U-Net architecture
embedding the EfficientNet architecture in the encoder part
and conjunction the same with the decoding part. Both the
classification and the localisation part fetched us state of the art

results. The work is capable for usage in practical applications.
However there is still scope of improvement in the image
localisation part.
