Abstract— Signature verification is an important aspect of
digital security in general and specifically in banking, legal, and
administrative transactions, where fraud protection and
identity authentication are paramount. Previous signature
verification technologies have been trained mainly on Englishlanguage documents and thus function with reduced capability
in multilingual noisy real-world settings such as in India. Such a
language/context mismatch may result in strong operational
issues, such as higher false rejection rates and lower overall
verification accuracy.

But like with all technological advancements, the majority
of the conventional signature verification systems have been
English-dominated datasets [7]. These systems perform
poorly when faced with documents that include local scripts
such as Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Urdu, and other Indian
languages [8] and, as a result, have greater false acceptance or
rejection rates. Lack of multilingual generalization ability
restricts the wider applicability of these solutions in diverse
real-world environments, especially in linguistically diverse
nations such as India.

These are to be addressed with an effective, modular deep
learning-based multilingual signature verification system
consisting of three autonomous but interconnected phases:
detection, cleaning, and matching. At the detection phase,
YOLOv5 is utilized for efficiently localizing signature areas in
documents with various scripts, supported by signature formspecific anchor tuning and aspect ratio optimization
adaptations. The cleaning step employs unsupervised K-Means
clustering to separate foreground signature ink from
background noise such as stamps, printed lines, and seals to
provide cleaner input for matching. Lastly, the matching step
employs a Siamese network with a VGG16 backbone to learn
discriminative feature embeddings and compare them by cosine
similarity, trained on contrastive loss to enhance intra-class
compactness and inter-class separability.

Apart from this, real-world document samples also add to
the complexity. Real documents typically introduce visual
artefacts like stamps, seals, overlapping printed texts, bleed
ink, and background noise [9]. The artefacts obscure signature
areas, and thus effective detection and extraction are difficult.
Simple thresholding-based and hand-engineered denoising are
not adequate enough to address such complexity effectively
[10].
For addressing these urgent issues, this paper introduces a
noise-tolerant, multilingual, and modular signature
verification system that is based on state-of-the-art deep
learning techniques. The suggested system breaks down the
verification pipeline into three specialist phases—detection,
cleaning, and matching—and enables independent
optimization of separate stages. YOLOv5 is utilized for strong
multilingual signature localization [11], K-Means clustering is
used for unsupervised denoising [12], and VGG16-based
Siamese network is used for strong signature similarity
estimation [13]. High scalability, multi-lingual adaptability,
and realistic deployability at enterprise-scale document
pipelines are facilitated with this module-based architecture.

Easily accessible experimental evaluation of real-world legal
and financial documents shows the efficacy of the system with a
mean Average Precision (mAP@0.5) of 92.7.
Keywords— Signature Verification, Multilingual Dataset,
YOLOv5, K-Means Clustering, VGG16, Siamese Network,
Document AI, Deep Learning, Forgery Detection, Contrastive

PROBLEM STATEMENT

Majorly, the majority of existing signature verification
models have been predominantly trained on Englishdominated data [19]. Consequently, these models exhibit poor
generalization when tested against documents containing
region scripts such as Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Urdu, etc.,
which are abundantly present in India [12]. Language-biased
training brings along much higher rates of false rejection and
limits cross-script flexibility, making such models infeasible
for deployment in linguistically rich environments.

Aside from straightforward verification, development in
document layout understanding has also been instrumental.
One of the proposed groups was LayoutXLM, a visually stateof-the-art document parsing transformer from different
languages and layouts [9], which is essential to enable
downstream OCR and detection pipelines. Multilingual OCR
methods were explored under low-resource conditions in
recent research, focusing on the lack of region-annotated data
[10]. A specially developed dataset for Indian regional
signatures was presented to enable stable training of
multilingual models [11]. Apart from that, huge surveys on
document image processing noise reduction methods listed
clustering-based methods as extremely effective [12].

METHODOLOGY

The system being discussed follows a formal three-step
procedure: Dataset Preparation, Signature Detection,
Signature Cleaning, and Signature Matching. Every step is
separately optimized in order to gain maximum overall system
performance in noisy multilingual document environments.
A. Dataset Preparation:
x English-only signature datasets were originally
employed to prototypize and test the baseline [19].
For breaking the English-biased model constraints,
the dataset was incrementally augmented by
synthetically generating multilingual documents [12].

C. Matching Phase:
x During this final phase, pre-processed signature
images are matched against one another by a Siamese
neural network with VGG16 backbone [15].
x

Cosine similarity between the embeddings is
computed to detect how similar two signatures are.

Cross-script testing validated consistent performance with
Hindi, Marathi, and Urdu signatures, questioning the
multilingual ability of the system. It was less robust with very
cursive signature writing styles, with over-inking or poor scan
quality, particularly for the case of the Urdu samples, where
there was not enough ligature spacing. The samples are
promising to further enhance with more advanced data
augmentation and adaptive preprocessing.
Perceptual vision assisted YOLOv5 in detecting signature
locations efficiently even remotely among dense document
content. Perceptive vision was strongly augmented when
background content was properly evaded during post-cleaning
scanning when performed for the first time. Basic adoption by
the K-Means algorithm was, however, in an indefensible
position to denoise with or without tagged guidance or
templating.
ROC curve analysis revealed ideal discrimination of actual
and pseudo signature pairs. The almost ideal ROC AUC of
0.984 speaks volumes about the discriminative potential of the
Siamese feature embeddings and efficiency of contrastive loss
training. There were nearly no false positives and false
negatives at test time, which signifies model consistency.
Besides, qualitative user experience on visual verification
tasks indicated pre-cleaning signatures by the cleaning
module as more human-readable to examiners, another
evidence of greater value in real use. Overall, modularity not
only yielded satisfactory quantitative results but also turned
out to be usable in real use, a prerequisite for successful largescale use in banking, jurisprudence, and administration.

B. Future Work:
Promising as is the present system, there exist some areas
to improve for future work:
x

Attention-Augmented Matching: Adding attention
blocks like the Convolutional Block Attention
Module (CBAM) to the Siamese model enables the
model to focus even more intensively on the most
important parts of a signature, making even better
matches.

x

Edge Optimization: Optimization for future
consumption to enable real-time on lower-end devices
will be tackled by techniques like quantization,
pruning, and low-resource-computation use like
MobileNet or EfficientNet.

x

Extended Language Support: Beyond existing
support for the four languages, other languages like
Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, and Kannada can also be
supported through some more multilingual synthetic
training data.

x

Hybrid Denoising Techniques: Hybridizing the
clustering algorithms like K-Means and denoising
algorithms like U-Net based on deep learning can
result in even cleaner signature extraction,
particularly of bad degraded or noisy documents.

x

Interactive Dashboard Development: There will be
created an interactive web-based dashboard where
there will be document upload functionality, visually
mark signatures found, similarity scores view, and
audit verification functionalities with explainable AI.

x

Explainable AI Integration: Integration of
explanation capability into the system will enable
end-users to see why two signatures are similar or
dissimilar, building user trust and regulation
compliance in highly regulated sectors such as
banking and government identification verification.
