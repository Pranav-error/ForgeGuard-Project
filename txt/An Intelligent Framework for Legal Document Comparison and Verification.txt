Abstract—Legal documents consisting of contracts, agreements, or certifications are considered the basis for many
decision-making processes, rendering the integrity of the documents to be of primary importance. Due to the rise in
digitalization in the field of law with its consequent growth in the
number of digital documents, manual verification of the
documents was considered to be a tedious, difficult, or inefficient
process prone to errors due to the involvement of the manual
factor. Especially detecting changes to the texts within the
documents or determining the legitimacy of the signature
associated with the documents is considered to be a tedious job
by itself for the concerned professionals dealing with documents
using the manual method. This article proposes an intelligent
system for matching the nature of the documents with the aim of
assisting the verification process by using the facility of artificial
intelligence for document verification by including the aspects
like natural language processing for the verification of the
documents with the help of the signature associated with the
document.The experimental evaluations performed using
different sets of legal documents verify the superiority of the
proposed approach in terms of high precision for finding similar
contents as well as signature irregularities. The results confirm
the potential of the system in developing an efficient solution for
improving the efficiency, reliability, and robustness of the
verification process for legal documents.
Index Terms—Legal document verification, document similarity analysis, natural language processing, signature authentication, semantic text analysis, machine learning, artificial intelligence, legal informatics.

I. INTRODUCTION
Legal documents include contracts, agreements,
certificates, and affidavits; these are, in fact, the very
backbone of any le- gal, financial, and administrative system.
Ensuring authenticity and integrity of such documents is
pivotal to avoiding fraud, unauthorized changes, and possible
related litigations. The fast digitization of legal records and the
ever-growing move toward electronic document exchange
have turned traditional manual verification processes
inefficient, very time-consuming, and prone to human error.
Therefore, automated and intelligent methods that are able to
verify legal documents with high accuracy and speed have
become increasingly in demand.
Advances in AI have allowed the use of natural language
processing and computer vision methodologies on document
analysis. The semantic text similarity approach enables one to

contrast the contents of documents beyond just superficial
matches of keywords, which would allow for the detection of
paraphrased or structurally modified text.Transformer-based
language models achieve better performance with respect to
contextual understanding of legally complex language. Human
handwritten signature verification has been studied extensively
using both machine learning and deep learning approaches, and
convolutional neural networks achieve remarkable success in
extracting discriminative visual features from signatures.
Nevertheless, despite such advancements in automated document analysis, automated verification of legal documents still
proves to be an daunting task, particularly since legal documents
are highly structured and domain-specific in nature, where
clauses can be very complex in both meaning and lengths.
Furthermore, signature verification has proved to be an additional
complex process with issues of intra-writer variation, inter-writer
similarity, as well as skilled forgery. Typically, most of the
existing systems address issues of semantic text analysis and
signature verification individually or in isolation from each other,
producing incomplete or unre- liable results of document
verification in practical applications. The need for this paper is
based on the shortcomings of the existing system by proposing an
AI-based framework for semantic legal text similarity and
handwritten signature veri- fication. The proposed framework
can perform semantic text similarity for contractual clauses and
authenticity for hand- written signatures by utilizing a
transformer and CNN-based models, respectively. The proposed
framework can perform comprehensive verification by utilizing
joint text and signature authenticity. Thus, this proposed
framework can perform more
reliable and comprehensive verification for documents.

III. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND REQUIREMENT ANALYSIS A.
Problem Statement
This exponential growth of electronic legal documents
includes but is not limited to contracts, agreements, and
certificates and compliance records. It has been a result of
rapid digitization within workflows in the field of law. The
authenticity and consistency of such documents need to be
established, as even minor textual changes or forged signatures concerning such critical legal documents may lead to
serious legal, monetary, and reputational losses. Traditional
document verification for legality depends considerably on
manual inspection by a legal expert, which in turn makes the
entire process time-consuming, labour-intensive, and prone to
human error, especially in large-scale repository document
verification.
Most existing automated methods try to meet this challenge by either detecting textual similarity or performing
signature verification as a standalone task. For text-based
methods, keyword matching, and semantic similarity models
usually do not detect paraphrased or contextually altered legal
clauses reliably. On the other hand, approaches for signa- ture
verification, if performed in isolation, cannot consider
inconsistencies between document content and authenticity of
signing. When it comes to text, things like keyword matching
or semantic models often miss the mark—they struggle to
catch paraphrased or subtly changed legal clauses. Flip to
signature verification, and you’ve got another problem. If you
only check the signature without looking at the document’s
content, you can miss inconsistencies or even clever forgeries
that slip right through. Consequently, the central problem this
research tries to solve arises from the fact that a unified,

scalable, and intelligent framework for the verification of legal
documents by assessing semantic content consistency and
signature authenticity in combination is missing. Essentially,
there is a need for an automated approach that would minimize
reliance on manual verification while ensuring accurate,
consistent, and depend- able results across diverse legal
contexts.
B. Requirement Analysis
It needs to catch the true meaning behind the text, not just
match keywords. In legal writing, rephrasing or just moving a
clause can completely change what something means, so the
system has to understand context and meaning at a deep level. On
top of that, signature verification matters a lot. Signatures and
seals often prove whether a document’s real.
The system should spot forgeries whether someone tried to
quickly scribble a fake, carefully copied the signature, or
simulated it with sophisticated tools. It’ll need deep learning to
pick up on those tiny details, all while keeping false alarms—both
false positives and negatives to a minimum. It alsoneeds to handle
various document format types, such as image scans of PDFs and
editable text files, among others.
To do this, it needs to clean up images and use optical character
recognition (OCR), so it can turn scan-only documents into
something machines can actually read, while keeping everything
in place.
But here's the thing it shouldn't treat checking the text and
analyzing signatures as totally separate steps. Instead, it should
blend those results together and make one clear decision about
whether the text is genuine. From a non-functional perspective, it
needs to ensure that its design is both scalable and agile enough
to analyze a potentially large number of documents with rapid
response times, incorporating stringent security measures such as
access controls on different user roles.
Besides that, it needs to ensure design flexibility to cope with
constantly evolving forgery methods via its modular design
architecture. Lastly, despite its high level of automation within its
capabilities, its design needs to ensure that it is interactive to a
degree to ensure both its accuracy and trustworthiness for
forensics usage within the legal fraternity.

V. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
In this paper, an excellent methodology with excellent intelligent and integrated features has been instituted within the
process of verifying legal documents depending on semantic
textual similarity and autenticity of signatures. The major aim of
this methodology is ensuring that with a minimum use of
inspections, maximum accuracy exists within the process of
verifying legal documents depending on certain features. Unlike
other systems in use today, this method accepts both forms of
verifications as a whole within the subject matter of considering
semantic similarity and autenticity signatures separately from the
rest.
The methodology will start with the acquisition of the
documents. The documents may be in various forms because they
may be provided in the form of scanned documents or even PDF
and text files. Then there will be an element in the methodology
concerning the preprocessing of documents with the objective of
information extraction from the legal documents in the forms of
text and graphics. The information contained in the document will
be extracted depending on its semantic content by using the
method of high-level information processing in the form of
natural language processing. Similarly, the region for the
signature extraction will be performed with the objective of
validation using high-level image processing method.
Among what can be considered as integral issues of what is
called the proposed methodology is that it is going to present an
integration or fusion of what is determined from both the textual
similarity component and signature verification component of
that methodology so that it encourages that no falses will be
present in verification, thus encouraging scalability in diverse
legal spaces, particularly where they are used independently of
each other.

X. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
In this study, we developed a system to verify the authenticity
of legal documents. We didn’t just stop at one step; we put
together several pieces — first, we clean up the documents to
make their formats consistent, then we dive deeper and analyze
their meaning, and finally, we verify the signatures. The results
really highlight how much this integrated approach can help
both in catching errors and in reducing the usual hassle people
face with document verification.

To really put the Legal Document Similarity Matching System
While going through its paces, various legal documents including
contracts, agreements, certificates, among others, were used.
However, this was not the only type of document used during the
process, as the database was carefully designed to be reflective of
actual cases. This meant that apart from using the genuine legal
documents, their paraphrases, alterations, and even forgeries were
also used. The forgeries in some instances went further and
involved faking signatures in order to make them as realistic as
possible. It had to be comprehensive because the system is
supposed to perform flawlessly in any circumstances.
For the purposes of actually analyzing documents, however, we
opted for using state-of-the-art Natural Language Processing
tools, particularly models based on transformers. In all honesty,
transformer models can do wonders when it comes to interpreting
legal language nuances. Traditional approaches, where only
word-by-word similarities would be searched, were completely
out of the question; transformers can go much further than that,
being capable of recognizing clause relations, detecting
paraphrasing, identifying transitions between legal language
forms, and spotting overlapping and contradictory clauses.
However, our efforts did not end there. In addition to analyzing
text, the system we implemented also takes advantage of
Convolutional Neural Networks. Consider CNNs as highly
trained vision – they excel at pattern recognition and anomaly
detection. In the context of this project, a CNN was specially
developed to help identify signatures. Rather than simply looking
for similarities in signatures, the neural network will identify
patterns in the strokes used, their structure, and general
composition.
The greatest advantage of our methodology is that everything is
interconnected. The model itself does not solely analyze text but
merges NLP for comparing texts with CNNs to verify signatures.
Such an integration makes the entire process more reliable and
complete. At the same time, while NLP focuses on identifying
similarities and differences between texts, CNN concentrates on
verifying signatures.
