Abstract—As computers grow older, some vital documents
such as identity credentials, certificates, documents of law etc
are stored and transferred electronically and this exposes them
to forgery. Traditional human verification is inefficient, prone
to mistakes and cannot be used in mass-scale to verify
documents. To address those issues, we recommend a hybrid
method of document forgery recognition based on the
combination of Error Level Analysis (ELA), Convolutional
Neural Networks (CNNs), explainable AI, Optical Character
Recognition (OCR), and encryption-based logging. ELA-CNN
module identifies the possible points of tampering and GradCAM provides visual justification to enhance the
interpretability . OCR is used to retrieve the key text fields such
as names, dates, and identification numbers which are then
checked by regular expressions to verify semantic accuracy [3].
Metadata and the outcome of detection is securely stored and
encrypted to offer tamper-evident records and auditable
records [4]. Tests that have been conducted using a benchmark
corpus are demonstrating that the proposed system can detect
high accuracy in several types of forgery with transparency,
semantic authentication, and secure storage and can be applied
to banking, education, governance, and insurance cases.

This has inspired the creation of ForensicDocAI a hybrid
automated signature verification system that includes: A deep
residual network (ResNet-50) used to do classification, which
has been fine-tuned. Explainable analysis using Grad-CAM
to interpret forensic information, SHA-256 and RSA/AES
hybrid cryptography implementation, and Audit trail storage
of verifiable records based on blockchain.

II. LITERATURE SURVEY
Offline signature verification has evolved to the statistical
handcrafted feature engineering to the fully automated deep
learning-based recognition. Early attempt was based on
geometry stroke analysis, frequency breakdown and pen
trajectory simulated[11] , [3]. Nevertheless, these techniques
were based on manual feature extraction, which made them
have little flexibility between writers and environments.
Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) were introduced and
greatly increased verification performance because it allowed
automatic extraction of features directly out of pixel data.
Such architectures include VGGNet [15], Siamese networks
[15], and residual networks [1] exhibit high levels of
generalization especially when fine-tuned on large-scale
models[2].The latest publications are focused on the
transparency of the system and hybrid verification systems.
This is of particular importance where the results of the
models have an impact on legal or compliance-guided
decisions; methods like Grad-CAM are useful in visualizing
feature salience maps that illustrate the decision making
process [6].

III. THEORETICAL BACKGROUND
Offline signature verification is an image processing,
machine learning, cryptography and secure storage
technology. The suggested system is based on three
theoretical elements of deep learning as a feature extractor,
binary classification, and integrity protection through hashing
and blockchain.
A. Convolutional Neural Networks.
A neural network (a convolutional neural network (CNN)) is
used to extract significant visual features like the pressure of
a pen, the smoothness of a curve, the distance between
curves, and the continuity of strokes. The essence of the
process is the convolution of an input image.

F. Blockchain-Based Tamper-Proof Verification
The proposed system has blockchain technology to guarantee
non-repudiation, traceability and immutability of signature
verification records. Once the authenticity of a signature is
established a cryptographic hash of the validated document
and classification metadata, including timestamp and
confidence score is produced. The smart contract records this
hash as a transaction in a ledger of blockchain such that
verification records cannot be changed once they are written.
The environment of a private Ethereum-based blockchain is
deployed with the help of the Ganache framework, which
allows controlled and low-latency confirmation of
transactions. The smart contract logs the value of the hash and
the result of the verification thus producing a tamper-evident
audit trail. In further verification requests the previously
stored hash of the document would be retrieved on the
blockchain and compared to a newly calculated hash to verify
integrity. Any mismatch is a sign of the unauthorized
modification. The proposed system will offer long-term trust,
auditability, and safe validation that are essential in legal,
financial, and institutional authentication situations by
combining blockchain and deep learning-based signature
verification

Figure 1 demonstrates the topology of the proposed system,

The offered methodology was based on combining deep
learning-based classification and secure verification to
guarantee accuracy and tamper-resistant document
validation. The processing flow starts by obtaining images of
the signatures offline and then proceeds to process the image
by removing noise, converting the image to grayscale, and
normalizing the size of the image to less than its original size
256×256, and contrast adjustment. These measures help to
maintain consistency in different quality of input and writing
styles. Upon preprocessing, the images are then passed to the
ResNet-50 trained classification model which learns
discriminative features and determines authenticity by
comparing the learned representations based on the stroke
width, writing rhythm, curvature patterns, and differences in
spacing. The result of the classifier is a binary answergenuine or forged, as well as a confidence score, which
indicates confidence in the model.
To increase the system transparency and trust, explainability
layer, based on the Grad-CAM visualization, is used to
identify the signature regions which affected the
classification decision. This gives visual justification to
decisions particularly where there are ambiguous cases.
There is the classification step after which, cryptographic
hashing is used to create a digital fingerprint of the certified
signature that is unique. The resulting hash, including
classification metadata is stored in a blockchain network
securely through a smart contract deployed on a local
Ganache environment. This provides immutability,
traceability and long term validation of verification results. A
blockchain look up is activated by any further verification
request, where the stored hash is compared to an additional
computation, and this is used to verify document integrity and
to avoid replaying or forgery attacks.A smart contract is also
used to store the hash of the generated hash and verification
metadata on a blockchain ledger permanently to provide
tamper-proof storage and auditability.

VI. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
An analysis with a curated dataset of real and forged offline
signatures was done to evaluate the proposed system. Once
the ResNet-50 model was trained with fine-tuning, the system
should reach a consistent result on both accuracy and
precision as well as recall and F1-score measures. The last
accuracy was 97.8% which shows that the model can
generalize to different handwriting styles as well as levels of
forgery. The rates of false acceptance and false rejection were
also much lower than baseline CNN models, and that is why
the relevance of residual learning framework and
preprocessing pipeline is justified. The Grad-CAM
visualization also helped to strengthen the interpretability of
the classifier, by demonstrating that the decisions made by it

From Table III provides a comparative analysis of different
signature verification systems based on their architectures,
datasets, and key advantages. Traditional methods like
Template Matching rely on hand-crafted features and are easy
to implement but limited in scalability. The SVM with HOG
approach introduces feature engineering, allowing better
generalization across varied signatures. SigNet, built using a
Siamese CNN, performs well in identifying writerindependent features.

The suggested system exhibits a viable architecture of safe
offline signature verification with the incorporation of deep
learning-based classification, explicability, and blockchainsupported storage. ResNet-50 was able to differentiate
between real and fake signatures with high scores on
evaluation metrics, which means that it can effectively extract
features and generalize them. Grad-CAM visualization,
which was incorporated in the model, also contributed to
improved transparency in the decisions made by the user
since the crucial areas of the stroke were highlighted, which
is especially significant in the context of the legal or auditlevel justification of the decisions made. Besides, the SHA256 hash and blockchain ledger system allowed making
verification outputs unalterable, identifiable, and resistant to
tampering. These elements form a reliable verification chain,
which can be used in institutional, judicial and commercial
authentication scenarios where reliability and integrity of
data are important.
Although the findings are encouraging, the framework has
the potential of improvement. Future studies can cover the
use of hybrid or transformer architecture to enhance
sensitivity of features particularly in handling highly skilled
forgeries or degraded input samples. More broadly,
interoperability between different organizations that could be
federated as a single system and deployed on public or
consortium blockchains could be made possible through the
extension of the system to trigger federation and
interoperability between different entities. Others such
directions are the integration of multimodal biometrics, realtime verification interfaces, adaptive explainability models,
and the high-level adversarial defense strategies. The
proposed system can be improved to become a scalable and
standardized solution of secure digital authentication in
various application domains with these advancements.
