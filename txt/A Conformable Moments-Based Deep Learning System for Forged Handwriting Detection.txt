A Conformable Moments-Based Deep Learning
System for Forged Handwriting Detection
Lokesh Nandanwar, Palaiahnakote Shivakumara , Senior Member, IEEE, Hamid A. Jalab , Rabha W. Ibrahim,
Ramachandra Raghavendra , Senior Member, IEEE, Umapada Pal , Senior Member, IEEE,
Tong Lu , Member, IEEE, and Michael Blumenstein , Senior Member, IEEE

Abstract— Detecting forged handwriting is important in a
wide variety of machine learning applications, and it is challenging when the input images are degraded with noise and
blur. This article presents a new model based on conformable
moments (CMs) and deep ensemble neural networks (DENNs) for
forged handwriting detection in noisy and blurry environments.
Since CMs involve fractional calculus with the ability to model
nonlinearities and geometrical moments as well as preserving
spatial relationships between pixels, fine details in images are
preserved. This motivates us to introduce a DENN classifier,
which integrates stenographic kernels and spatial features to
classify input images as normal (original, clean images), altered
(handwriting changed through copy-paste and insertion operations), noisy (added noise to original image), blurred (added
blur to original image), altered-noise (noise is added to the
altered image), and altered-blurred (blur is added to the altered
image). To evaluate our model, we use a newly introduced dataset,
which comprises handwritten words altered at the character level,
as well as several standard datasets, namely ACPR 2019, ICPR
2018-FDC, and the IMEI dataset. The first two of these datasets
include handwriting samples that are altered at the character
and word levels, and the third dataset comprises forged International Mobile Equipment Identity (IMEI) numbers. Experimental
results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms the
existing methods in terms of classification rate.

I. I NTRODUCTION

I

T IS noted from previous work [1] that developing systems
for detecting suspicious activities is gaining popularity in
the field of machine learning and forensic security applications
[2], [3]. This indicates that the alteration or tampering of content in images is a serious threat for forensic applications, such
as forging property, insurance, certificates, banking documents,
and other sensitive documents such as creating fake suicide
notes and fake answer scripts. In addition, there are several
occasions, such as gender, writer, nationality identification, age
estimation, behavior identification using handwriting analysis, which can assist forensic investigation teams to identify
criminals. In these situations, altering, tampering and forging
of content can have tragic consequences, and it may even
cause death of a person. Therefore, robust forged handwriting
detection, which is invariant to noise and blur, is essential.
There are methods available for detecting forgeries in document images, video, and general images [4], [5], [6], [7], [8],
[9], [10], [11], [12]. Most of the methods rely on image
features such as texture, color, and distortion at the edges
for detecting forgeries in the images. However, these features
may not be adequate for detecting forgeries in handwritten document images due to large variations in the writing
[13], [14]. To overcome this limitation, a few methods have
been developed that work based on inconsistency and irregular
patterns of forged texts [15], [16]. But these techniques are not
robust to handwritten documents affected by noise and blur
because of inconsistencies and irregular patterns, which are
used as a cue for forgery detection.
Moreover, it can be observed that handwritten documents
are comprised of words affected by noise, blur, text with poor
quality, and degradations due to errors in devices, flaws in
the process of image acquisition, paper quality, and camera
movements while capturing images. The factors make the
problem of forged handwritten text detection in noisy and
blurred environments complex and challenging. In addition,
the forgery at a character level adds more complexity to
the problem. This is because character-level forgeries do not
provide noticeable differences compared to word and text lines
forgery for detection. Thus, there is a need for addressing the

Therefore, we define six classes, namely: 1) Normal words

created through standard copy-paste and insertion operations;
3) Noisy words where different noise densities are added to
the original image; 4) Blurred words where the original image

Overall, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first work
focusing on forged handwriting detection in noisy and blurred
environments.
The key contributions of the proposed work are as follows: 1) conformable moments (CMs) are employed here for
enhancing the finer details in images irrespective of degradations introduced by forgeries, noise, and blur; 2) a new deep
learning model is developed by exploring the combination of
stenographic kernels and spatial filters; 3) to the best of our
knowledge, the existing forgery detection methods have not
explicitly considered noisy and blurred input images. Thus,
this work is new in this respect; and 4) since it is the first
of its kind, the creation of a dataset with its ground truth for
forgery detection is a cumbersome task. We have done this,
and we shall release the code and dataset publicly to support
research reproducibility.
The remainder of this work is structured as follows.
A review of the related methods of fraudulent document
identification, source printer identification, and forged text
detection is presented in Section II. Section III introduces a

proposed work.
II. R ELATED W ORK
In the past decade, we can see extensive research being
undertaken for fraudulent document identification, source
printer identification, and forged text detection in document images. The methods can be classified into two broad

categories, namely genuine document identification and printer
identification.
Loc et al. [17] proposed an approach for securing genuine documents by exploring adversarial networks. It embeds
watermarking to hide secret information in the document.
However, the method is sensitive to distorted documents.
Berenguel et al. [18] proposed a method for detecting counterfeit documents based on a deep learning model. Due to the use
of high-quality printers, fraudulent documents were expected
that did not leave any noticeable mistakes. However, the work
assumed that when a counterfeit document is created, some
abrupt changes in the background texture of the document
would be evident. Raghunandan et al. [4] aim to identify
fraudulent handwritten documents by considering them as a
two-class problem. The approach uses Fourier coefficients for
studying the quality of handwritten documents. Quality-based
features may not be robust for documents affected by adverse
factors, such as distortions, noise, blur, and forgery operations.
The above review on fraudulent document identification
methods show that most approaches use documents that do
not suffer from degradations, poor quality, and aging for
forgery detection. This is because the methods work on the
assumption that the forgery operations introduce distortions.
This assumption may not be true for documents with noise,
blur, and degradations.
In a similar way, there is another set of methods that
identifies the source printer for detecting forged documents.
Wang et al. [12] propose a method for printer identification
based on different texture features and passing extracted features to an SVM for the classification of documents printed
by different printers. Bibi et al. [19] focus on developing a
method that identifies the source printer, which is text independent. To achieve this, the approach uses deep convolutional
neural networks (CNNs) in contrast to hand-crafted features.
Joshi and Khanna [20] propose a method for source printer
classification by defining local texture descriptors. However,
the extracted features are sensitive to document aging and
degradation. One can note from the review on the methods
of source printer identification that none of the methods
uses handwritten documents for altered text detection. This is
because the methods expect unique noise or distortions created
by different printers.
Recently, Shivakumara et al. [21] targeted forged International Mobile Equipment Identity (IMEI) number detection by
exploring color features and fusion concepts. Similar to [21],
Nandanwar et al. [16] propose an altered text detection method
in document images. It considers PDF documents for altered
text detection. The method uses the input image and the
image reconstructed by DCT for finding differences between
input and reconstructed images. To address the challenges
of forged handwritten documents in noisy and blurry environments, Kundu et al. [15] proposed a method based on
pixel distributions corresponding to the Fourier spectrum and
its shape. Although the method is robust to blur and noisy
words, it does not perform well for character-level forgeries
and altered-noise, blurry images. Megahed et al. [22] proposed
a method for forgery detection in handwritten documents
based on ink color features. These features are extracted

propose a new architecture based on stenographical kernels
and spatial filters [28] for extracting such unique patterns.
III. P ROPOSED M ETHOD
Since there are several powerful word segmentation
approaches that work well irrespective of script, orientation,
degradation, and distortion [29], [30], [31], we consider words
as the inputs for the purpose of altered text detection. The
proposed work considers six classes as discussed in the introduction section namely: 1) Normal words; 2) Altered words;
3) Noisy words; 4) Blurred words; 5) Altered-Noisy words;
and 6) Altered-Blurred words.
Inspired by the fact that CMs can find solutions to complex
problems such as nonlinear complexities [24], [25], we explore
CMs for altered text detection. It is expected that CMs provide
enhanced images for the six classes by preserving the finer
details [26], [27] of characters. This results in the unique
edge patterns for all the six classes, which can be used for
the classification of altered words from normal, noisy, and
blurred words. Motivated by stenographical kernels and spatial
kernels, which help us to extract contextual information with
the help of local spatial information, we propose to design
a DENN. This approach integrates stenographic residual features, and features extracted by the CNN from photograph
montages for detecting altered images from other images.
The proposed method extracts statistical features using
Stenographic Residuals with High Pass Filters for both input
and enhanced images given by CMs. At the same time,
to increase the strength of stenographic residual features,
we generate a photograph montage using inputs and CM
images from which the proposed method extracts Spatial
CNN features using the EfficientNet [32] backbone trained
on the ImageNet dataset as a feature extractor. The motivation

A. Theory of CMs
As we have discussed earlier, when we perform forgery
operations on an input image in noisy and blurry environments,
the quality of the images degrades. When the quality degrades,
a variance increases between the distortion created by the
forgery operation, and the noisy, blurry handwriting variations.
Therefore, to cope with this challenge, we introduce CMs to
improve the quality of the image, spatially. In the case of
CMs, the basis function with an alpha power is important to
enhance the edge pixel values. It is stated due to Anderson and
Ulness [25] that the CMs are derived from Conformable Calculus (CC) based on control theory to estimate the performance
of the proportional-differentiation controllers in terms of a
well-known error function. We explore this idea to formulate
a new CM and derive interesting results by establishing a
relationship between the class of CC and control theory and
use it for altered text detection. We briefly present a summary
of CMs here to convey the idea of the operation using CC.
Details about CC can be found in [25].
Overall, fractional calculus reduces the influence of backgrounds and distortion, while moments preserve spatial information of the finer details (edges) in the images such that one
can expect unique patterns for each of the classes.

B. DENN for Classification of Altered Handwriting
In the case of handwriting, pixel values and their neighboring values play a key role in defining a spatial relationship between pixels, which can be used to extract structures
of characters. In our work, the altered images affected by
forgery operations, noise, and blurred images, are considered
for classification, which affect the structures of characters.
At the same time, since a normal word is not affected by
any forgery operations, noise, and blur, the character structures are not affected, and hence the word is not affected.
In this work, it is expected that forgery at the character level,
and the alteration caused by forgery operations, may not be

5411

noticeable. These are the observations for classifying images.
Inspired by the special properties of Stenographic kernels that
are popular for extracting the spatial dependency of pixels,
which can be named as spatial context features, we explore
stenographic kernels through residuals using different orders
of high-pass filters [28]. We extract stenographic features
by merging several smaller residuals of high-pass filters and
then concatenating all the residuals to obtain the feature
vector. Therefore, stenographic features are the combination
of sub-models known as residuals.

DENN Architecture.

To integrate the stenographic features and CNN-based features, we propose a DENN Architecture as shown in Fig. 6,
where one can see how the proposed method combines both
the features. The stenographic features extracted from input
and CM images results in a feature vector of size n, which
is then passed through the fully connected neural network
(F-CNN-1). It consists of six fully connected (FC) layers.
The final layer of this FC network contains 16 neurons.
Conversely, the features extracted using the efficient net CNN
backbone from the montage of inputs and CM images are
fed to a fully connected neural network (F-CNN-2) having
global max pooling (MP) and three FC layers. The final layer
of F-CNN-2 has 16 neurons. The DENN fuses the outputs of
F-CNN-1 and -2 and subsequently passes through the FC layer
to the final output layer (multilabel classification) containing

We use the cyclical learning rate (CLR) [37] strategy to
train the proposed architecture. CLR dramatically reduces
the number of experiments required for tuning and finds an
optimal learning rate. This strategy allows the learning rate
to oscillate back and forth between upper and lower bounds
when training, slowly increasing and decreasing the learning
rate after every batch update. This leads to an optimal way of
improving model performance. To find the upper and lower
bounds, we have utilized the same approach as defined in
CLR, where we train our network for a small number of
epochs with a very low learning rate, such as 1e-10, which
starts increasing our learning rate exponentially after each
batch update. Training will continue until we reach a very
high learning rate (1e + 01).
Testing the plot of smoothed loss over time enables us
to determine the lower and upper bounds of the learning
rate. When the learning rate is large enough for the loss to
decrease, it is considered as a lower bound of the learning
rate. When the learning rate is too large to the point where
the loss starts to increase, it is considered as an upper bound
of the learning rate. For this work, the upper bound of the
learning rate is determined to be 5e-2 and the lower bound is
found to be 1e-5. Then the proposed method employs CLR
for 96 epochs for a step size of 8 epochs and a batch size
of N = 16 images, leading to 6 full CLR cycles to oscillate
back and forth between upper and lower bounds. For all the
experiments, we use a system with the Nvidia Quadro M5000
GPU for training and testing of the architecture and a python
framework along with TensorFlow-v2.1 for this application.
The datasets are divided into 80% and 20% for training and
testing for all the experiments in this work. The parameters
used in this work, namely image size, the number of FC layers,
neurons, batch size, learning rate, etc., used in this work are
determined experimentally. The system takes approximately
1.5 min for each epoch on the proposed dataset, for a total of
2 h 20 min to complete all the experiments.
To analyze the time complexity of the proposed method,
we considered the following key steps: 1) CMs enhanced
image; 2) stenographic feature extraction; and 3) deep CNN
ensemble for classification. Since the parameters of the CMs
depend on the number of overlaps in the blocks of a ×a pixels,
the time complexity for an image is O(n) in the worst case,
and the same for the best case, which is (n). It is noted that
the time complexity of stenographic feature extraction for an
image is O(n 2 ), where n is the number of pixels in the image.
Therefore, the time complexity for extracting Stenographic
features from the image is O(n 2 ) in the best case, and O(n 3 )
in the worst case. In addition, the time complexity for the
classification step using a Deep CNN ensemble is (n). In
summary, the overall time complexity of the proposed method
in the best case is O(n) + O(n 2 ) + O(n) ⇔ O(n 2 ), and in
the worst case is O(n) + O(n 3 ) + O(n) ⇔ O(n 3 ).

To demonstrate that the proposed enhancement is effective and

these existing methods is to improve poor-quality images. The

issues of poor quality. To show that fractional calculus alone is
not sufficient to handle the challenges of altered text detection
in handwritten images, we compared the proposed method
with the existing methods. Similarly, the method [43] which

learning model alone is not adequate to improve the quality
of handwritten images in noisy and blurry environments.
For altered handwriting detection, to show that the proposed method is effective and useful, we implemented the
following existing methods for a comparative study in this

forged IMEI number detection. Kundu et al. [15] proposed
Fourier spectral density for forged handwritten word detection in noisy and blurry environments. Megahed et al. [22]
proposed ink color-based features for handwriting forgery
detection. Nandanwar et al. [23] explored DCT-phase statistics for altered air ticket detection. The reason to choose these
methods for our comparative study is that they share a similar

altered and original, but not for the six classes used in our

V. C ONCLUSION AND F UTURE W ORK
In this work, we have proposed a novel method for altered
handwriting detection in noisy and blurry environments based
on CMs, and a DENN. In order to preserve structures of characters in handwriting images, irrespective of noise, blur, and
large handwriting variations, we have introduced a novel CM
model involving Fractional calculus and geometric moments.
The proposed CMs enhance the finer details such as edges in
images of six classes, namely normal, altered, noisy, blurred,
altered-noisy, and altered-blurred. To extract edge patterns
in all the images of all the classes, we have proposed a
new deep architecture that combines features extracted from
stenographic residuals of the input and the CM images,
and a photograph montage. Experimental results on different
datasets, such as our dataset, the standard ACPR-2019 dataset,
the ICPR 2018-FDC, and the IMEI datasets demonstrate that
the proposed CMs and the altered detection approach outperform the existing methods. When the single image affected
by multiple forgeries (copy-paste and insertion), the proposed
method may not be effective. This will be considered as part
of our future work.
