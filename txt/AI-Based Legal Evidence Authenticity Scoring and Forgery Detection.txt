Abstract— The use of scanned and digitally stored documents
in legal, government and administrative systems has raised the
possibility of tampering and forging documents. The traditional
manual verification approaches can be slow, subjective and fail
to identify any manipulations at the pixel level. In order to
overcome these shortcomings, this paper proposes an automated
system of document authenticity verification, combining deep
learning algorithms with image forensic verification. The
proposed system uses Error Level Analysis (ELA) to detect the
compression inconsistencies that can be used as evidence of
tampering, and it is refined by a convolutional neural network
(CNN) to detect the exact location of the forged areas at a finegrained level. Besides, metadata consistency validation like
document creation date, history of modifications, and
information about devices are also conducted in order to increase
the forensic reliability. The major input to the system will be a
visual forgery heatmap that shows suspicious areas, and an
objective Authenticity Score of 0 to 100 that measures the
chances of document integrity. Integrating visual interpretability
with numerical evaluation, the suggested method will give
qualitative and quantitative data, which will support scientifically
valid and legally admissible decision-making. The system is also
expected to minimize human error, enhance the efficiency of
verification as well as a reliable digital document validation
system to be used in real life legal and administrative application.
Keywords—Image Forensics, Deep Learning, Legal
Document Verification, Convolutional Neural Network, Error
Level Analysis, Document Security, Authenticity Scoring.

A. Passive Image Forensics
The passive image forensics is the study of the natural
marks created in the process of manipulation which do not
imply the presence of any embedded watermark or the digital
signature[1][4]. The idea that is constructed out of these
techniques is that real pictures have certain statistical features
which are disturbed when they are perturbed[1][4].
These are some of the tools of the passive form of
forensic
analysis that are typically used, the Error Level
Analysis (ELA)[1]. ELA exploits compression artefact of the
JPEG compression and an image of a particular quality is
saved and the disparities between the pixel values are made.
On the aspects of the manipulated images, there exists the
asymmetric distribution of errors as opposed to original areas
in the case of manipulation happening in the course of the
process[1][2]. The equation of ELA map E is calculated as: E

Our proposed system entailed the automated legal documents
verification forensic system, which incorporated the U-Net
segmentation and metadata analysis and the ELA-based
feature enhancement. The F1-score of the system is 92.5%

systems[12][19]. Both the visual heatmap and the

workflow strengths.

that the human knowledge is given attention to the
documents marked.

quantitative scoring instead of the subjective knowledge.
Admissible evidence: Heatmap gives a pictorial description
to the forensic finding.
