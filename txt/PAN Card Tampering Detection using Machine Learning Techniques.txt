Abstract— In an era dominated by digital transactions and an
increasing reliance on electronic identity verification, the need
for robust security measures has become important. As people
and

identity fraud has become a serious concern. The machine
learning-based PAN card tampering detection system offers an
innovative solution to address the growing issue of identity fraud
and document manipulation, particularly within the financial
and government sectors. The dataset consists of 40 real and 40
fake PAN cards and also 20 real and 9 fake Aadhar cards. The
system uses CNNs, a specific type of deep learning model
intended for image processing, to mitigate this problem. The
performance of CNN is also compared against various pretrained models like ResNet50, VGG16, Xception and

that CNN is the best performing model with an accuracy of
100%, loss 0.14% and average execution time of 0.24%. It
meticulouslyanalyzes any discrepancies and variations such as
alterations intext, wrong numerical data and tampering with the
photos. The incorporation of CNN into this novel system not
only resolves the existing vulnerabilities related to PAN cards but
also lays the groundwork for an identity verification
environment that is both more secure and reliable in the context
of financial and governmental transactions.

CNNs follow a specific architecture, having several key
components that will replace the human brain's capacity in
recognizing patterns and features in images. Convolutional
layers, pooling layers, fully connected layers, and ReLU
activation functions are some of these constituents.
Convolutional layers generate the image input by use of
filters or kernels. Such layers enable the network to detect
specific patterns or images such as lines, curves, or shapes.
The model becomes non-linear when the ReLU activation
function is used. The model can now learn more intricate
patterns thanks to it. The input's spatial dimension is reduced
using pooling layers. This helps to lower the model's
computing cost and hence avoid overfitting. Finally, fully
connected layers allow the network to use the observed
features to forecast the input.

II. RELATED WORKS
This research study highlights the challenges associated with
identity fraud and document tampering, emphasizing the
vulnerabilities inherent in traditional methods of document
verification. Notably, the utilization of machine learning,
specifically CNN has gained prominence in addressing
similar challenges in various domains.
The work in [1] highlights the critical role of feature selection
in fraud detection techniques of credit card using genetic
algorithms to optimize feature selection and SMOTE to
address the issue of data imbalance. It also does not address
techniques to adapt to evolving patterns. Fuzzy membership
functions are proposed as a solution to the data imbalance
issue in [2] and the privacy concerns around the use of credit
card transaction data are not discussed in detail. The research
in [3] allows several parties to cooperatively train machine
learning models without disclosing their private information
to one another. It also addresses the data privacy concerns by
utilizing encryption techniques such as SMPC and
homomorphic encryption. Privacy preservation is not
discussed about in [1] and [2]. In [4], the study utilizes
decision tree classifiers as one of the primary methods and
addresses privacy and security concerns by suggesting
strategies such as data anonymization and secure data
handling.

In [13], researchers have proposed a new method to
distinguish documents produced by laser printers and inkjet
printers using SVM by separating individual characters and
determining the origin ofthe document based on the aggregate
vote from decisions on individual characters. The accuracy
reached 90% for inkjet printers and most laser printers and
copiers and was robust for JPEG compression. In [14], have
proposed a copy-move forgery detection technique utilizing
AKAZE features and RANSAC for eliminating false matches
and the robustness outperformed SIFT and SURF based on
manipulators like white gaussian noise, rotation and JPEG
compression. In [15], have presented how to detect duplicated
regions in natural images using DWT which can only detect
translationduplicate forgery and KPCA which can detect both
translation-flip and translation-rotation duplications and also
achieves the highest true positive rates with false positives.

In [5], researchers focused on fraud detection in credit cards
using Local outlier factor and Isolation Forest algorithms and
the result of this research indicates an accuracy of 99.6% but
with a low precision of 28-33% due to imbalance between
valid and fraudulent transactions. In [6], to handle the credit
card dataset imbalance issue, Matthew’s correlation
coefficient is identified as a strong measure for this issue and
the SMOTE method helps in improving the performance of
the classifiers. In [7], blockchain networks can be used to
enhance security when it comes to credit card transactions
and employs SHA 256 to secure data at each stage and uses
Random Forest (RF)to optimize model accuracy. The study
in [8] employs ANN for fraud detection in credit cards as it
outperforms other models like SVM and KNN in terms of
performance metricslike accuracy, precision and recall.
In [9], the study shows the usefulness of RF in image fraud
detection as it overcomes the risk of overfitting and achieves
an accuracy of 90% in classifying fake cards. The drawbacks
of SVM, cluster analysis, and Naive Bayes classifiers are also
addressed. In [10], after comparing the performance of
various models to detect credit card fraud, SVM, KNN and
CNN excelled individually using the European dataset,
ensemble methods combining KNN, SVM, and Random
Forest yield improved MCC and AUC using the Australian
dataset and SVM, Random Forest, and CNN excelled

The PAN card dataset was tested on four other ML models in
addition to CNN to check the efficiency of the dataset.
1) VGG16: This is the CNN model that uses 13 convolution
and 3 fully connected layers. It is known for its simplicity and
effectiveness. The image size is (150, 150, 3). This model
consists of 4 layers. The first layer is the base model which is
the VGG16 model with 14,714,688 parameters. The second
layer is the flattening layer which converts the 4x4x512
feature maps from the base model into a 1D array. The third
layer is the first dense layer with 512 neurons that uses the
ReLU function as the activation function with 41,94,816
parameters. The fourth layer is the second dense layer with 1
neuron that uses the Sigmoid activation function with 513
parameters.

employed for this system. Convolutional, pooling, and fully

2) ResNet50: This is a type of CNN model which uses
residual blocks in its architecture that introduce skip
connections that have been observed to reduce the vanishing
gradient problem. Residual blocks consist of multiple
convolutional layers, typically with filters of 3x3. The image
size is (150, 150, 3). There are a total of 4 layers. The first
layer is the base model, the ResNet50 with 23, 587, 712
parameters. The second layer is the flattening layer which
converts the 5x5x2048 feature maps from the base model into
a 1D array. The third layer is the first dense layer that has 512
neurons with the ReLU activation function and the second
fourth layer is the second dense layer with 1 neuron that uses
sigmoid function as the activation function.
3) MobileNetV2: It is a CNN model that uses the concepts of
residual blocks that helps preserve information flow while
using a minimal number of parameters and computations.
The image size is (150, 150, 3). There are a total of 4 layers.
The first layer is the base model, the MobileNetV2 with
2,257,984 parameters. The second layer is the
AveragePooling2D. The third layer is the first dense layer
that has 512 neurons with the ReLU activation function and
the second fourth layer is the second dense layer with 1
neuron that uses sigmoid function as the activation function.
4) Xception: The spatial and channel-wise convolutions are
divided into two distinct processes by Xception using a
depth-wise separable convolution algorithm. The image size
is (150, 150, 3). There are four layers in this model, the base
model layer, the AveragePooling2D layer, the first dense
layer which has 512 neurons and uses ReLU as the activation

in comparison with the Aadhar card dataset as the larger
volume of diverse images which included changes in name,

more comprehensive results compared to the Aadhar card
dataset which limited the scope of experimentation.
