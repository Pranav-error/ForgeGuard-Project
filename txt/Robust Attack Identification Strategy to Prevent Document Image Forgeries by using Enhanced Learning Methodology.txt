Abstract— Forgery in images is a long-standing issue that

spans back to ancient times. Throughout history, images
have been employed as evidence or documentation of
events. In the present day, the accessibility of image
preprocessing tools and affordable hardware has
facilitated the creation of manipulated images with ease,
often driven by ulterior motives such as disseminating
false information or pursuing personal gains unlawfully.
In recent years, the spotlight has turned to Artificial
Neural Networks (ANNs), sparking significant interest in
the realm of image forgery identification. Nevertheless,
prevailing image forgery techniques rooted in ANNs are
often specialized, geared towards identifying distinct
types of forgeries. Consequently, a pressing need arises
for an approach adept at efficiently and accurately
uncovering unfamiliar forms of image manipulation. This
research fills that gap by presenting a robust deep
learning-based system designed specifically to spot fakes
while working with double image compression. The
model's training hinges upon discerning disparities
between an original image and its recompressed
iterations. To enhance the model's performance, the Ant
Lion Optimization (ALO) technique is employed to finetune the features of the input data. Noteworthy for its
efficiency, the proposed model stands out as a lightweight
solution. Empirical results underscore its prowess,
showcasing swifter effectiveness compared to prevailing
state-of-the-art technique. Encouragingly, the model
achieves an impressive overall validation accuracy of
95.87%, reinforcing its efficacy in identifying image
forgeries.

Keywords: Image Forgery Detection, Artificial Neural Networks,
Ant Lion Optimization (ALO) technique, Document Image
Forgeries.

III. METHODOLOGY
A computer model that mimics the structure and
behavior of real neural networks, such those found in the
human brain, is called an ANN. It plays a crucial role in the
deep learning as well as machine learning processes. ANNs
are designed to process and learn from data, enabling them to
perform tasks like pattern recognition, classification,
regression, and more.
The artificial neuron, sometimes called a node or
perceptron, is the network's fundamental element. Neurons
take in data, process it, and then send it out as a signal.
Typically, neurons in a neural network are arranged in several
layers:

Input Layer: This layer receives the initial data or
features that are fed into the network.
Hidden Layers: These levels are intermediate
among the input as well as output levels. In order to
discover useful patterns and characteristics from the
incoming data, they execute sophisticated
computations. The depth of these layers contributes
to the term "deep" in deep learning.
Output Layer: The network's ultimate output is
generated at this layer, and it might take several
forms (such as a categorization label or regression
value).

The intensity of the signal sent from one neuron to
another is based on the weight assigned to each link between
neurons. The neuron also has an associated bias that affects
its output. The network's non-linearity is introduced when the
weighted sum of inputs and bias is fed into an activation
function. The sigmoid, the hyperbolic tangent (tanh), and the
rectified linear unit (ReLU) are all examples of activation
functions that are commonly used.

A. Ant Lion Optimization (ALO)
The ALO algorithm is modelled by the catching
technique used by ant lions, which involves digging conical
trenches to ambush their prey. The program attempts to
identify the best possible solutions to optimization issues by
modeling their behavior on that of ant lions and ants.
Although ALO is often used to optimization problems, it has
found usage in other areas, such as forgery detection.
It is important to keep in mind that ALO is not as
well-known or as widely used as other optimization
algorithms in the machine learning and image processing
fields, such as genetic algorithms, particle swarm
optimization, or gradient-based optimization techniques.
There may not be a well-documented standard strategy to
utilize ALO in this context, and as of my last knowledge
update in September 2021, its acceptance and
implementation in specialized tasks like forgery detection
may be restricted.

Ant Lion Optimization Implementation: Implement

Local Search and Movement Rules: Define how ant

Validation and Evaluation: Use a validation dataset

The proposed approach makes advantage of the
feature image that is the contrast among the original as well

fraud since it is easy to see that the faked component of the

have been able to get quite precise results. On the other hand,
these methods' low quality in detecting picture fraud stems
from the fact that they search for it at the pixel level, where
false positive pixels are reported and therefore lower the
overall accuracy of the forgery detection at the image level.

range of quality settings. In addition, we have shared the
findings of our testing of the proposed model using several
different JPEG quality criteria. Accuracy is maximized by
maintaining a quality factor of 90 or above. The proposed
approach enhances precision by making use of more
sophisticated input features, as opposed to systems that
merely employ the original image as input characteristics. To
achieve this, in addition to the enhanced processed
characteristics, we also supply the results of training our

methods by a wide margin when it came to identifying forged
images. Furthermore, our approach can deal with copy-move
as well as spliced-image forgeries. However, only a small

predictions much more quickly than before. This means that
our concept has practical applications.
