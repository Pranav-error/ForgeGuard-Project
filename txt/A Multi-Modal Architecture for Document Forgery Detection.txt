Abstract—Document fraud has evolved into a technically
sophisticated threat, involving high-resolution print–scan recapture, selective region manipulation, and adversarial rewriting.
This work presents a multimodal deep architecture for robust
forgery detection that integrates visual texture cues, structural
edge patterns, and textual semantics. The system employs an
MobileNet-based visual backbone augmented with CBAM and a
lightweight ASPP module to capture ﬁne-grained manipulation
artifacts. Parallel pathways model structural inconsistencies via
edge analysis and linguistic deviations through OCR embeddings,
targeting splicing and font substitution attacks respectively. The
three modalities are fused through a normalized integration block
and classiﬁed using an MLP head. Experiments conducted on a
10k-image IDNet-Estonia-derived corpus demonstrate an accuracy of 97.8 and an AUC of 0.9992 across seven fraud categories.
Qualitative visualizations further conﬁrm the model’s ability
to localize manipulation artifacts, signiﬁcantly outperforming
unimodal baselines in complex attack scenarios.
Index Terms—Document Forgery Detection, Multimodal Deep
Learning, MobileNetV2, Optical Character Recognition (OCR),
Edge Detection, CBAM Attention Module, Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP), Image Tampering Detection.

IV. M ETHODOLOGY
A. System Overview
The proposed forgery detection framework integrates
three complementary information streams—visual appearance,
structural boundaries, and textual semantics—to capture a
wide spectrum of manipulation cues commonly found in
identity documents. Each modality is processed by an independent branch designed to extract features relevant to its
domain. The resulting embeddings are fused into a uniﬁed
representation and classiﬁed using a multilayer perceptron.
This design enables the model to jointly reason about pixellevel noise patterns, geometric inconsistencies, and OCR-level
content deviations.

III. DATASET
A. Dataset Origin
The dataset used in this study is derived from the Estonia subset of the IDNet benchmark dataset (IDNet–EST),
a publicly available corpus designed for document forgery
detection and identity manipulation researches [15]. Each
document instance in IDNet comprises one genuine sample
and six systematically generated forged variants, corresponding to distinct fraud categories: copy-and-move, inpaint-andrewrite, face morphing, portrait substitution, direct text-ﬁeld
replacement, and combined forgery patterns.
Each document instance is organized into separate directories, where each folder represents a speciﬁc forgery category.
This structured organization enables precise segmentation of
forgery types and facilitates controlled experimental evaluation
for both classiﬁcation and veriﬁcation tasks. Notably, both the
facial images and textual ﬁelds within the ID cards are entirely
synthetically generated, ensuring data privacy and controlled
forgery simulation [15].

All images are resized to 256 × 256 and normalized using
ImageNet statistics. During training, random ﬂips, small-angle
rotations, and color jitter are applied to simulate distortions
introduced by various acquisition devices.

A Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) is integrated to strengthen the network’s ability to focus on visually
relevant regions. CBAM reﬁnes the intermediate representation by applying channel and spatial attention successively,
enhancing sensitivity to subtle manipulation traces. As each
channel of a feature map is considered as a feature detector,
channel attention focuses on ‘what’ is meaningful given an
input image [13].
To capture multi-scale artifacts—often present at both
ﬁne (digit-level) and coarse (full-region) granularities—an
Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) module is employed. ASPP aggregates information across multiple receptive
ﬁelds, enabling the network to detect both local and global
inconsistencies[27].

E. OCR Branch
Textual manipulation is a prevalent form of document
tampering, commonly involving character rewriting, font substitution, spacing irregularities, and number replacement. To
capture such cues, an OCR-based branch processes characterlevel textual information extracted from document images.
OCR output is converted into a ﬁxed-length character
sequence, which is embedded into a 64-dimensional vector
space. An attention-based aggregation mechanism is then
employed to adaptively weight salient character representations. Speciﬁcally, a learnable query vector attends over the
embedded sequence using a scaled dot-product formulation,
producing a weighted summary representation. The aggregated vector is subsequently projected into a compact 32dimensional embedding:

vimg ∈ R1280 .
D. Edge Branch
Structural deformation is a common outcome of tampering
processes such as region replacement, boundary erasing, and
text overwriting. The edge branch focuses on these geometric
cues using the edge map E.
A lightweight convolutional network processes E through
a 7 × 7 convolution with stride 2 to capture coarse structural
patterns, followed by batch normalization, ReLU activation,
and spatial dropout for regularization. Global average pooling
is then applied, and the output is projected into a compact
32-dimensional embedding:

vtext ∈ R32 .
This attention-driven formulation enables the model to emphasize informative character patterns and typographic irregularities while suppressing noisy OCR artifacts, thereby improving robustness under real-world document degradations.

VII. C ONCLUSION
This work presents a multimodal deep-learning framework
for reliable document forgery detection by integrating visual
texture cues, structural edge patterns, and semantic inconsistencies extracted through OCR. The proposed architecture
combines a MobileNet-based feature encoder with a dedicated
edge-enhancement branch and a text-semantic veriﬁcation
module, enabling robust detection of print–scan recapture,
region-level manipulation, font tampering, and textual rewriting.
Experimental results demonstrate that fusing low-level manipulation artifacts with high-level semantic context significantly improves generalization, particularly on challenging
real-world forgeries. Overall, the proposed system offers a
practical and scalable solution suitable for educational institutions, veriﬁcation agencies, and government workﬂows
requiring automated document integrity veriﬁcation.
VIII. FUTURE SCOPE
Although the proposed model demonstrates strong performance, several avenues remain for further enhancement:
• Real-World Dataset Expansion: Increasing the dataset
size with diverse, high-resolution government and institutional document categories would improve robustness
under varied printing, scanning, compression, and lighting conditions.
• Transformer-Based Multi-Modal Fusion: Future extensions may incorporate Vision Transformers (ViT), Document Transformers, or multimodal fusion architectures
such as LayoutLMv3 or Donut to achieve more expressive
cross-modal reasoning.
• Fine-Grained Localization of Forged Regions: Enhancing the model with pixel-level manipulation detection—via Grad-CAM++, SAM, or integrating a U-Net
segmentation head—would enable explicit localization of
forged or tampered areas.
• Adversarial Robustness and Anti-Spooﬁng: Incorporating adversarial training, noise-tolerant layers, or
recapture-spoof detectors could strengthen the system
against highly sophisticated AI-generated or GAN-based
forgeries.
• On-Device and Real-Time Deployment: Optimization
through model quantization, pruning, TensorRT acceleration, or ONNX export may support deployment on
