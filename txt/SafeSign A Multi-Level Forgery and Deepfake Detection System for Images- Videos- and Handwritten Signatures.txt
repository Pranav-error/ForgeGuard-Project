Abstract- The fast development of artificial intelligence and
other digital media editing tools leading falsified images,
deepfakes, and falsified handwritten signatures have become
serious challenges and very difficult to handle in the field of
cybersecurity,
digital
forensics,
and
document
verification/authentication.
Manual
and
rule-based
verification methods are not always capable of identifying
this type of sophisticated forgery, especially those that are
produced using deep learning models. In this paper presents
SafeSign:AI-based multi-level forgery detection system that is
used to analyze and verify the authenticity of the images,
videos, and handwritten signatures in the single system. The
process of image tampering detection is done through
EfficientNet convolutional neural networks (CNN) that are
trained to detect inconsistencies that are added during
manipulation. The video deepfake detection technique is
obtained by frame-based analysis of the deep learning models
that identify visual artifacts in facial areas but with
computational efficiency. The verification of handwritten
signatures is done with the help of a Siamese CNN which is
trained to learn the similarity measure between authentic and
fake signatures. The suggested system is implemented as an
online application that enables its users to upload media files

I.

INTRODUCTION

The authenticity of digital media has become a critical issue
due to the intensive use of images, videos, and scanned
documents in social media, money transactions, court cases,
and online communication. Simultaneously, the fast
development of artificial intelligence and processing images
have simplified the manipulation of the digital content in the
most realistic forms. Phishing, fake video clips, and faked
signatures of a handwritten signature are becoming a
widespread method of fraud, misinformation, and identity

Visual contents can be changed using image tampering
methods, including copymove, splicing, and retouch methods
which do not create clear evidence, and with deepfake
technologies it is possible to create believable facial
manipulations in videos. Signatures that are written down,
still commonly used as a means of identity checking in banks
and legal documents, are also vulnerable to skilled forgery.
As a result, manual checking and rules-based verification
methods are not adequate anymore to uncover such complex
forgeries.
In order to overcome these limitations, there has been an
interest in automated forgery detection with machine learning
and deep learning. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)
have demonstrated overall high performance in identifying
manipulated images and videos through the training of
discriminative features of visual characteristics that are
directly learnt via data [2], [3]. Equally, frame-based and
spatio-temporal deep learning architectures have been
suggested to deepfake detection [4], and architectures based
on Siamese networks have been effectively used to identify
handwritten signatures [5],[6].
Although profound progress was made in the field of deep
learning-based forgery detection, the majority of the current
practices concentrate on one modality, i.e., image tampering,
video deepfakes, or handwritten signature verification alone.
Such single-mod focus restricts their usage in practice in
forensic and cybersecurity cases, where varying types of
digital evidence have to be considered jointly very often.
Moreover, very accurate models can be very expensive to
compute, which makes them less viable to use in real-time.
Therefore, an effective, scalable, and unified system that
would identify several types of forgery in digital formats is
required. This paper fills this gap by introducing the
SafeSign, a multi-level forgery and deepfake detection
system that incorporates image and video as well as
handwritten signature checks in the single deployable
platform.
II.

LITERATURE SERVEY

The phenomenon of digital forgery has been actively studied
because pf the rapid growth of artificial intelligence-based
content generation. Existing research mainly focuses on
individual forgery modalities such as image tampering, facial
deepfake detection, and handwritten signature verification.

Wang et al. [1] gave an extensive survey of passive image
tampering detection methods, classifying them into pixellevel, format-based, camera-based, and semantic-level
methods. Their paper pointed out that conventional
handcrafted forensic tools are powerful in identifying copy–
move and splicing attacks but are very delicate to postprocessing activities like compression and resizing, so they
are not robust in real world setting.
A large-scale benchmark of facial manipulation detection
with different levels of compression was introduced in
FaceForensics++ dataset by Rössler et al. [2]. Their
experiments showed that CNN-based models are much better
at detecting manipulated faces images as compared to human
observers. Nevertheless, the work is confined to the field of
facial forgery and other types of digital forgery are not
discussed.
D. Afchar et al. suggested MesoNet [3], a small CNN
structure that is trained on mesoscopic facial video forgery
detection. The model was both competitive and had low
computational complexity to ensure that it was applicable in
near real-time. However, its performance was demonstrated
to decline on unseen datasets, which means poor
generalization.
O.de Lima et al. [4] investigated deepfake detection using
spatiotemporal convolutional networks through adding the
temporal data across video frames. Their findings showed
better results than frame-based methodology, but the
implementation of 3D CNNs is much higher, which is
inconvenient in lightweight systems.
Siamese network-based methods have been highly embraced
in the field of handwritten signature verification. H.
Hafemann et al. [5] evidence that similarity measures can be
successfully trained in Siamese CNNs between real and
forged signatures, whereas M. Soleimani et al. [6] indicated
that deep feature learning can be more successful than
handcrafted features in signature verification on the offline
setting. These are however limited to signature analysis and
not combined with other mechanisms of forgery detection.
The latest CNN-based image tampering detection tools [7]
have demonstrated higher robustness than traditional forensic
tools when they are trained on learning spatial anomalies. In
spite of their efficacy, such methods are usually image-level,
and are not extrapolatable to video and document forgery
detection.

METHODOLOGY

SafeSign is a system that uses a modular and systematic
approach to identifying various types of online forgery, such
as image manipulation, video deepfakes, forged handwritten
signatures, and so on. The processing of each type of media
is carried out with the help of a specific deep learning model,
and all the modules are combined into a single framework.
A. DATA PREPROCESSING
The input media files are never left untouched in their original
form as they are subjected to preprocessing to enhance
consistency and better model results. Frames of images and
video are rescaled and standardized to the input demands of
the corresponding convolutional neural networks. Video files
are run through to remove representative frames at predefined
intervals to trade-off between accuracy of detection and
computation. Images of handwritten signature are converted
to grayscale, scaled, and normalized to make strokes more

B. IMAGE TAMPERING DETECTION
Image tampering detection is done by means of a CNN-based
classification system with EfficientNet as the underlying
architecture. The model is trained to recognise genuine
pictures and manipulated ones by learning spatial
inconsistency which is introduced during manipulation.
Image forensic methods that have been developed by deep
learning have shown high robustness over traditional handcrafted feature-based methods, especially when subjected to

C. VIDEO DEEPFAKE DETECTION
The video deepfake detector module uses a frame-based
method of deep learning to make sure it is computationally
efficient. A CNN is trained to detect visual artifacts added
during the generation of deepfakes through the extraction of
facial regions of sampled video frames followed by their
analysis. The spatiotemporal models do not have this
drawback since they are more effective in capturing the
temporal inconsistencies at a high cost of computation [4].
Previous research has demonstrated that the frame-based
CNN models can perform competitively when the predictions
are made using the multiple frames [3], [9]. In line with this,
SafeSign combines frame-level predictions to generate an
effective video-level authenticity score.
The spatiotemporal deep learning architectures, which
include the 3D CNNs and CNN-LSTM, can capture the
temporal discrepancies between adjacent video frames, but at
a much higher cost in terms of their computational cost and
inference latency. By contrast, the suggested frame-based
CNN model processes representative frames at a set time
resolution and frames together with frame-level predictions
to generate a video level authenticity score. This design
provides a balanced trade-off between detection performance
and computational efficiency and hence, it can be deployed
in real-life forensic applications with fair level of real-time
performance shown in table.V.

D. SIGNATURE VERIFICATION
Verification of handwritten signatures is done with the help
of a Siamese Convolutional Neural Network which is
composed of two identical CNN branches that share weights.
Every branch derives feature embeddings of a true and a
suspected image signature. The distance between the two
embeddings is computed and the decision of authenticity is
ultimately made according to a predetermined parameter.
Siamese network-based methods have been extensively
verified to do offline signature verification because they are
capable of learning writer-independent similarity metrics [5],
[6].
E. DECISION AND REPORTING
In the case of each analyzed media file, the module provides
the prediction of authenticity and the score of confidence.
These findings are put together in the system to produce
computerized forensic reports. Receiver Operating
Characteristic (ROC) analysis during the process of threshold
calibration is used during the validation in order to establish
dependable decision limits, which is commonplace in
biometric and forensic verification systems [10].
The selection of decision thresholds used in authenticity
classification was done through the analysis of the Receiver

V.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

SafeSign system has a multi-layered and modular design which
is meant to offer the concept of multi-level forgery detection of
images, videos and handwritten signature on images in the same
platform. The architecture also reduces the difficulty of scaling,
maintaining, and processing the information because
application logic, model inference, user interaction and data
storage are divided into different layers such as Fig 2.

Application Layer: Application Layer is the heart of the
orchestration component of the system and implemented with
a Streamlit web application. It accepts the uploaded files by
the client layer in form of the HTTP request and manages the
general workflow. Depending on the form of input media, the
application layer forwards the data to the corresponding
detection module. It also handles the preprocessing, makes
calls to the trained models to do inference, aggregates results
and organizes report generation and logging.
Model Layer: The Model Layer is made up of the three
individual deep learning models that are specialized in
detecting a particular forgery task:
Signature Model: This is a Siamese Convolutional Neural
Network, which compares actual signature images and
suspected signature images and calculates a similarity score
to verify authenticity.
Image Model: The model is an EfficientNet-based
Convolutional neural network that is trained to detect
authentic or tampered images based on the learning of spatial
discrepancies.

VII.

CONCLUSION

This paper showcased the SafeSign, a multi-tiered forgery
and deepfake detection multi-level neural network trained to
combat the increased numbers of falsified images, videos,
and handwritten signatures. The proposed framework
incorporates EfficientNet-based image tampering detection,
frame-based video deepfake analysis, and Siamese CNNbased signature verification, which integrates into a coherent
framework, and the results are a high detection performance
and computational performance. Explicit testing showed that
there is good performance regardless of the modality and the
overall accuracy of 92.9. SafeSign is only lightweight and has
automated reporting, which is used to make it fit real-world
digital forensics and cybersecurity-related applications. The
future work will consider adding more modalities like audio
deepfake detection, and also deploy the system in edge
devices to enable low latency forensic analysis.
