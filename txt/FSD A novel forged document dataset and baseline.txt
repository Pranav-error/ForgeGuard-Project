Abstract— Forgery detection in multimedia is an
emerging research area in the field of digital content
security. The current research focuses on finding several
solutions to digital image forgery detection but a small
number of works have been reported for scanned
document forgery detection. The reason would be the
unavailability of a forged scanned document (FSD)
dataset. A scanned document can be manipulated in
different ways: deleting a region, copy paste a region of
the document either in the same document or in another
document. Detection of these manipulations becomes
arduous when a post-processing operation is performed
on the manipulated region. To advance research in this
field, we created a large dataset for the FSD. To generate
such a dataset, we collected scanned documents from the
FUNSD dataset (Publicly Available). The resolution of
each sample of the dataset is of size 755x1000. The
samples of the dataset are forged using different postprocessing operations. The dataset comprises 6656
instances of manipulated scanned documents with their
ground truth masks. To build the baseline for forged
scanned document detection, we evaluated state-of-theart object segmentation algorithms on the FSD dataset.
Experiments demonstrate that the FSD well represents
forged scanned documents and is quite challenging.
Keywords—Security, Digital Document, Authentication, Deep
Learning

I. INTRODUCTON
Forgery detection in scanned documents refers to
localizing the forged region in the digital document.
Localization of the forged region in scanned documents is
almost like localization of forged region in digital image and
object segmentation of computer vision. However, the
properties of the forged digital image are totally different than
the properties of a forged scanned document. Thus, a dataset
of forged digital images (natural scenes) can’t be used for the
evaluation of forged scanned documents. A visual difference
between a forged digital image and a forged scanned
document is shown in Fig. 1. The digital image has foreground
objects and a background. They are of different colors, shapes,
and different edges of the objects, etc. However, in scanned
documents, there are texts which are of very small edges and
with dense information. So, they both are very different in
nature with their properties and features. Also, a method to
detect forged regions in a digital image or detection of an
object in an image can’t be directly applied to detect forged
regions in a scanned document. The reason is obvious and
understood by an example: a little child who doesn’t know,
how an apple or pear looks like. What is the color and shape
of an apple or pear? Then he/she can’t differentiate between
an apple and a pear.

A limited number of solutions have been reported on
document forgery detection [1]–[5]. Most of these solutions
are statistical or conventional machine learning-based
methods and validated on a very small number of samples.
The cause is the lack of availability of large-scale datasets. In
addition, the current research in any field focuses on deep
learning techniques which require datasets of large scale. The
verification of learning models trained on a small-scale dataset
will lead to overfitting. Thus, we can summaries the following
challenges,
• Most of the techniques are based on the assumption
that tampered regions have some distortion (i.e., noise
or blur). However, if a forger is using a high-quality
scanned document and copies a region without any
post-processing operation, there is a possibility that
tampering processes don’t introduce any distortion.
• Techniques are not trained on the accurate dataset. For
example, MIDV-500 [6] dataset is for identity
documents that are not scanned document datasets.
• Existing works are not trained or validated on forged
scanned document datasets. This results in many false
positives.
Except for the aforementioned problems, detection
methodologies have challenges like post-processing
operations on copy-move or spliced forged regions. Internal
properties and features of scanned document images are
different from features of natural images captured using
digital cameras. Therefore, it stands to reason that forged
region detectors learned from natural images or identity
documents are not suitable for scanned document images.
To overcome the above challenges in forged document
detection and for wide and in-depth research we generate a
large dataset dedicated to forged documents only. We collect
some original scanned documents from FUNSD [7] dataset
and use Adobe Photoshop [8] to generate a forged document
with their annotated ground truth mask. The original scanned
documents are printed documents on an A4 sheet which are
scanned through the scanner and collected as FUNSD dataset.

We observe that forgery is possible in multiple ways on
scanned documents too. Based on this observation, we tamper
scanned documents in the following ways• Copy-Move Forgery- In this type of document forgery,
a region of a document is copied and pasted into the
same document intentionally to hide any information
about the document or to misguide someone with false
information.
• Splicing of document content: The content of one
document is copied and pasted to another document.
• Resampling of information: Information is resized in
the form of either downscaled or upscaled.

II. MOTIVATIONS
The importance of datasets can’t be overstated in the case
of data-driven techniques [9]–[14]. It is the task of a deep
learning or machine learning model to learn features from a
large variety of instances of a dataset. When it comes to the
object segmentation task of computer vision area there are
many datasets available in the public domain. These large
datasets like [15], [16], and [12], [13] are being actively
supported in the research community. If we talk about the
datasets of forged images, then datasets like CoMoFoD [17],
CASIA [18], IEEE-IFS [19], and CUD [20] are very
remarkable datasets, which are being used in abundance in the
research community.
There is a scope for improvement in the research of
detection and localization of forged documents which is
affected due to the unavailability of a dataset. According to
the forensic experts group [21], alteration to the pages or
content of the document includes an example of tampering
with the document. Since documents are highly used for
verification and authorization, the increased use of tampering
in such contents questions the integrity of such content.
Hence, the detection of the altered region in the scanned
document is a need of the world. Therefore, the unavailability
of a scanned document dataset and detection benchmark being
close to real-world application motivated us to work in this
research direction.

• We also benchmark state-of-the-art object

The second section of the manuscript discusses
motivations and related works. The third section gives a
detailed discussion about the annotated dataset and how it is
generated. The fourth section defines experimental work and
a baseline on the proposed dataset. The work is concluded in
the fifth section with future research direction.

A good benchmark dataset should have the following five
characteristics:1) A large number of instances to train and
validate a detection model. 2) Proper annotation of the forged
region in the ground truth mask. 3) Different categories of
forgery. 4) Each category (type of forgery) should have
enough instances and 5) Instances should have pre-processing
and post-processing operations on the forged region that make
it approach real-world application.
A dedicated dataset for identity documents is developed
by Arlazarov et al. in 2019 [6] called MIDV-500. It is having
500 video clips for 50 different identity document types with
ground truth. Similarly, Sidere Et al. generate a dataset in
2017. This was again for only identity documents. A method
for text line forgery is developed by Van Beusekom J et al.
[22]. This methodology was evaluated on five different types
of datasets which is generated from different printers and at
different depths. Although, these datasets are not available in
the public domain which lags the research in this domain. In
2021, a new dataset is developed by Mahfoudi et al. by
introducing copy-move forgery in payslip documents.

Most of the datasets have a smaller number of instances.
These datasets have limitations- number of instances, type of
forgery, or unavailability in the public domain. The published
datasets are in the form of handwritten or identity documents
and these datasets scanned can’t be used for the printed
scanned document as they give a piece of wrong information
related to the forged region or forged document. Keeping all
these limitations and challenges, we decided to develop a
dataset that should have a large number of instances with
different types of forgery.
III. ANNOTATION OF FSD DATASET
A. Image Collection
Deep learning models mimic human brains and almost
depend on training. So, the overall dependency of deep
learning models is either on the dataset or the architecture. A
dataset of forged document data is very important to train the
model and to the best of our knowledge, none of the forged
digital document datasets exists online in the public domain
(from where a model can learn). Hence, a dataset having a
different type of forgeries is constructed. The FSD dataset1
can be accessed after taking permission from the authors.
Some pre-processing operations and geometrical
transformations have been applied to the forged region in
documents which effectuate the multitudinous of this dataset.
This dataset is generated from the existing FUNSD dataset,
which is dedicated to text detection, optical character
recognition (OCR), spatial layer analysis, and form
understanding. This dataset contains 149 documents for
training and 50 document images for testing purposes. From
these documents, we have fabricated 3284 forged documents
and picked out 144 original documents as it is for the nontampered document. All presented documents and ground
truth in the constructed dataset are in png format. Dedicated
directories are created for the training and testing of the model.
One more directory is created for authenticated documents
that are not tampered with in any way and can be used to

B. Category of Forgery
Three types of basic forgeries have been constituted to
construct a dataset: (i) Copy-Move Forgery- In this type of
document forgery, a region of document is copied and pasted
in the same document intentionally to hide any information of
document or to misguide someone with false information. (ii)
Splicing of document content: The content of one document
is copied and pasted to another document. (iii) Resampling of
information: Information is resized in the form of either
downscaled or upscaled. Along with these forgeries, four
different pre-processing/post-processing operations and three
different geometric transformations have been carried out
over the forged documents. Details of operations implemented
over the forged region using Adobe Photoshop are as follows
(measure for post-processing/pre-processing operations are
used of Adobe Photoshop Application):
• First is a simple forgery in which a region is copied from
one document and pasted to the same document itself
(copy-move) or another document (splicing) without any
pre-processing or post-processing operations.
• The second is brightness enhancement of the forged region
of the document up to 100 points on the scale.
• The third post-processing operation is contrasted
stretching up to 100 points on the scale. This operation is
implemented in a forged region like brightness
enhancement.
• The blurring of the forged region is the fourth operation
that is brought out up to two neighboring pixels.
• In the fifth operation, white Gaussian noise is added up to
20% to the forged region of the document.
• Except for these image processing operations, three
geometrical transformations are carried over the forged
region. Among these three operations, the first
transformation is translation.

C. Dataset Splits
It is important to distribute the dataset into training,
validation, and test set. To ensure the training, validation, and
test result, we distribute the dataset into two parts- one is the
training set, and another is the test set. The training set can be
further divided into training and validation sets based on the
algorithm’s criteria. So, it is a researcher who is responsible
for the further division of training sets into training and
validation sets. We are providing the dataset in the public
domain. We evaluate the dataset on a high computing server
and set a benchmark on predefined object segmentation
models.
IV. EVALUATIONS AND BASELINE

baseline, we take different object segmentation and deep
learning models. On these standard deep learning
segmentation models, we train our proposed dataset and find
the training and validation evaluation results. For the
evaluation and benchmark, a high-performance computing
server is used. A continuous job pool is given to this server
where the training of the model is done one by one. We take
four different standard object segmentation models. These
models are Unet [28], Unet++ [29] and Unet-attention [30].

V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
The current research on digital content security focused on
detecting forged images, whereas very limited works have
been reported in the literature for forged document detection.
Such works evaluated their model’s performance on a very
small scale of forged document datasets. But the model can
overfit if they are trained on a small dataset. Thus, to address
such issues, this paper proposed to develop a largescale
dataset for a forged document which will be available with the

link given in the footnote on request. Several challenges are
also included in the dataset. These are in the form of postprocessing operations on the forged region such as noise
addition, rotation, scaling, brightness change and contrast
adjustment. The proposed dataset contains a total sample of
6656, out of which 60%, 20% and 20% of the dataset are
considered for training, validation and testing respectively.
Some of the state-of-the-art approaches have been coded and
evaluated on the proposed dataset to find out the criticality of
the proposed model. The highest accuracy on the proposed
