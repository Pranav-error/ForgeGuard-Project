Abstract—In Advancement of digital world, the increase in
the pace of issuing the digital certificates has also escalated the
threat of fake educational qualifications, including in
blockchain-based systems of verification, where the
immutability cannot be enough to eliminate the registration of
forged documents. In order to overcome this drawback, this
research suggests an AI-enhanced blockchain-based certificate
verification architecture that combines a hybrid deep learning
model and the ElimuChain architecture. The proposed
structure uses CNN-BiLSTM model that is able to identify
visual and semantic inconsistencies in the certificates before
anchoring them to the blockchain thus avoiding the possibility
of forged certificates being stored permanently. The checked
certificates are then hashed, hosted on the IPFS and
permanently recorded on the blockchain through the smart
contracts. Experimental assessment on a combined dataset of
true and fake certificates proves that the proposed model has an
accuracy of 98.3, precision of 97.9, recall of 98.6 and an F1-score
of 98.2, which is better than standalone CNN and Transformerbased baselines. Trust, security, and reliability of nationwide
systems of certificate verification are greatly increased based on
the integration of AI-driven pre-validation.
Keywords—Blockchain, Educational Certificate Verification,
Hybrid Deep Learning, CNN–BiLSTM, Certificate Forgery
Detection, Smart Contracts, IPFS, Artificial Intelligence,
Decentralized Applications, Trust Management.

INTRODUCTION

Educational certificates are important in verification of
academic performance and professionalism of individuals. As
the dependence on credentials to obtain a job, higher
education, and a professional license grows, the integrity of
certificates is turning into a burning issue on the global level.
Even after the utilization of digital record management
software, the issue of certificate forging has been ongoing,
which has been facilitated by the sophisticated image editing
software and the use of advanced document manipulation
software [1], [2]. According to the recent research, the forged
academic credentials still jeopardize the trust in the education
systems, the labor markets, and the institutions, especially the
cross-border ones [3]. The idea of using blockchain
technology as a way of issuing and verifying certificates has
gained a possibility of being a viable solution to the problem
because of its decentralization, immutability, and resilience to
tampering features [4], [5]. Certificates systems based on
blockchain allow the verifiers to verify credentials without the
help of centralized authorities, thus minimizing fraud, delays
in verification, and administrative overheads [6].
Nevertheless, the majority of solutions that currently exist in

the field of blockchain mainly assure post-issuance integrity
with the understanding that the certificates posted to the
blockchain are automatically authentic [7].
In recent works, an essential weakness of blockchainbased certificate verification systems has been pointed out:
blockchain is not able to identify forged or manipulated
certificates prior to registration [8], [9]. When a forged
certificate has been hashed and stored on-chain, it becomes
immutable, and thus makes fraudulent data permanent. The
resulting issue is the so-called garbage-in, garbage-out issue,
which is a massive constraint of the efficiency of blockchainbased verification systems in practical applications [10].
Concurrently, artificial intelligence and especially deep
learning have shown high performance in forgery of
documents through the analysis of visual features, text
structure and semantic anomalies [11], [12]. Hybrid deep
learning models Hybrid deep learning models combining
convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with sequential
learners have been demonstrated to be more effective in
document authentication tasks [13]. Nevertheless, currently
used AI-based systems to detect forgery are autonomous and
do not have trusted, auditable, and decentralized storage
solutions. Although this has been improved recently, there are
still three major challenges remaining: Unidirectional nonhacks Lack of pre-issuance forgery detection in blockchainbased certificate systems. Non-existence of smart systems to
ensure that forged certificates are anchored on-chain
permanently. Weakly coupled AI-based trust validation and
decentralized and nationalized certificate validation systems
[14], [15]. These dilemmas lower the reliability of the systems
and limit mass usage of blockchain-based educational
credential validation.
This research will solve these limitations by presenting a
proposal of an AI-enhanced blockchain certificate verification
system that expands Elim Chain system with a hybrid CNNBiLSTM deep learning model to detect forged certificates
before registration in the blockchain. The suggested
framework will implement a smart pre-validation layer, which
scans the certificate images to identify the visual and semantic
anomalies prior to the creation of cryptographic hashes to
store in IPFS and anchor them in blockchains. The key
contributions of this study can be summarized as follows: An
AI-enabled certificate verification system that can work with
hybrid deep learning and blockchain-based credential
management on a seamless basis. CNN-BiLSTM forgery
detecting model with a high success rate in detecting
manipulated certificates prior to the on-chain storage. A

II.

RELATED WORK

A. Certificate issuance and verification with the help of
blockchain.
It has been suggested extensively that blockchain can be
used to offer tamper-resistant anchoring of the provided
credentials, as well as, to streamline the third-party
verification. They can be systems on university levels or
national systems; a majority of the stores store certificate
fingerprints (hashes) on-chain and full documents off-chain
(e.g., IPFS) to minimize costs and enhance scalability.
ElimuChain is an example of a nation-level design, which
includes RBAC, government/regulatory enrollment, on-chain
revocation and IPFS storage to facilitate multi-level education
ecosystems [16].More privacy-sensitive versions have also
been designed recently (such as ZK-enabled academic
verification systems) to prevent the disclosure of sensitive
properties and still enable verification to be checked [17, 24].
B. AI-based detection of document / certificate forgery.
Individually, deep learning models have been shown to be
useful at detecting manipulated document images by training
to identify small differences in textures, fonts, signatures and
layout. More efficient cues (multimodal forgery cues) (CNN
+ sequential modules (BiLSTM or Transformer layers)) are
presented by classic CNN architectures and enhanced hybrids.
Hybrid CNN BiLSTM systems have also demonstrated stateof-the-art performance on adjacent detection problems (e.g.,
fake-news, document authentication), and exhibit greater
resilience to varied kinds of manipulations and also layout
changes [18]. Recently applied literature on the subject of
academic document forgery detection boasts of a high
accuracy rate with the utilization of domain-specific pipelines
that integrate visual processing and low-resource
hashing/checking steps [19].
C. AI and blockchain integration (new trend).
It is increasingly being understood that blockchain is
insufficient to ensure that forged documents are anchored in a
manner that is impossible to alter, and therefore, solutions to
the problem propose the introduction of pre-issuance AI
validation to eliminate garbage-in/garbage-out issues. Early
integrated systems have already shown viability (AI prevalidation + blockchain anchoring), and other directions have
been applying blockchain with verifiable ML primitives (e.g.
ZK proofs of model inference or models outputs) to bring
auditability and privacy to ML-driven checks [20, 23].
D. Privacy, scalability and distributed model training.
Credential systems Privacy-preserving protocols (i.e,
zero-knowledge proofs (ZKP), federated learning (FL)) are
becoming more commonly adopted: ZKPs can be used,
without revealing the content, to verify that a piece of data is
genuine, and FL can be used to train a common model across
institutions without a central repository of sensitive data.

Recent prototypes and surveys indicate that privacy leaks can
be addressed by ZKP and layer-2 integrations and that FL can
be applied to distributed fraud detection systems to learn
cross-institution forgery signatures without releasing raw
certificates [21,22, 25].
E. Gaps in research
Pre-issuance forgery detection is uncommon to blockchain
processes.
The vast majority of blockchain certificate systems (even
many operational prototypes) presuppose that uploaded
documents are authentic and only provide immutability after
they have been issued. This introduces a necessary weakness:
when a forgery is hashed and stored in blockchain, the forgery
will be forever (garbage-in immutable garbage). ElimuChain
captures the infrastructure of systems to use in national
implementation but fails to integrate AI validated in advance
with the issuance pipe.
Hybrid visual models plus sequential are not well
exploited in certificate authentication. Pure-CNN detectors:
Visual artifacts Sequence models: Text/layout semantics
Hybrid systems (e.g. CNN BiLSTM) may concurrently
capture spatial representations and sequential textual/layout
representations, however, little published research can be
found to apply such hybrids specifically to the detection of
educational certificate forgery on scale. The existing forgery
detection articles addressed single institutions or neighboring
areas (e.g. academic documents or fake-news), which means
that hybrids can be customized and thoroughly tested on
multi-format certificate archives. Here, there is a deficiency of
verifiable AI + blockchain pipelines that do not infringe
privacy.
New initiatives are investigating using ZK to validate the
output of ML or construct ZK-enabled verification systems,
but the connection between certificate issuance processes and
ZK is early. Systems that can generate verifiable proofs
indicative of an ML check passing (without leaking certificate
contents) prior to on-chain anchoring would be much more
trusted and simultaneously remain privately oriented, a
combination that is difficult to achieve with the literature. The
field of cross-institutional learning without the exchange of
raw data has not been fully investigated in detecting forgeries.
Patterns of forgery may differ among institutions, nations and
templates of certificates. Privacy/regulatory A centralized ML
training is frequently not possible. Principally, federated
learning solutions to distributed forgery detection have not
been thoroughly tested, where certificate datasets are
heterogeneous, class imbalance, adversarial manipulation, and
institutional drift are realistic.
There are operational issues (latency, compute, regulatory
workflow) that are open. National roll-outs need to strike the
right balance between the compute and latency added by AI
checks and user experience and cost (e.g. mobile/edge vs.
cloud inference). Published assessment of the combination of
end-to-end metrics, namely, detection effectiveness, false
positive effect on honest issuance, latency of verification, onchain savings of costs by avoiding fraudulent anchoring, and
governance procedures of disputed cases, is limited.

Recent blockchain-based certificate verification systems
have notably enhanced trust and transparency through
decentralization and irreversibility; still, their functionality is
still minimal, as it is summarized in Table 1. Early models are
mainly based on credentials anchored on blockchains without
mention of governance or revocation. Later researchers added
role-based access control and on-chain revocation to improve
institutional accountability, however, they are limited to postissuance integrity checks. Privacy-concerned schemes that
work with zero-knowledge proofs provide selective disclosure
of credentials, but do not provide smart validation.
Simultaneously, deep learning-based forgery detection
models show a high level of performance in forging
certificates, but they are not combined with blockchain
infrastructures, which means that they are used in isolation.
ElimuChain system goes a step further to offer the state of the
art by enabling management at the multi-level certificate
management with RBAC and on-chain revocation [16].
However, it still fails to stop forged certificates being
registered forever. Table 1 has indicated that the proposed AIenhanced system has the unique characteristics of combining
hybrid deep learning-based pre-validation with blockchain
verification, privacy preservation, and collaborative learning,
which removes the essential gaps in the current response.
III.

METHODOLOGY

Fig. 1 shows the architecture of the proposed framework,
which is a continuation of the ElimuChain infrastructure with
the addition of an intelligent pre-validation layer using hybrid
deep learning. The design is made in such a way that authentic
certificates are permanently imprinted on the blockchain to
overcome the inherent weakness of the blockchain systems to
identify forged inputs before registration. The system is
composed of five key elements, namely (i) authorized
certificate issuers, (ii) AI-based forgery detector, (iii)
decentralized off-chain storage based on IPFS, (iv) blockchain
network with smart contracts, and (v) certificate verifiers.
These elements are communicating with each other by means
of a decentralized application (DApp) that coordinates issuing
certificates, validating and storing them and verifying them in
a secure and scalable way.

A. Submission and Preprocessing of Certificate.
Schools that are approved by regulatory bodies make
submissions of digital certificates via the decentralized
application (DApp). All the certificates are submitted in PDF
or image format with the metadata, including the name of the
candidate, the ID of the institution, the type of award, and the
date when the award was issued. Before analysis, certificates
are preprocessed in order to standardize the inputs to the deep
learning model. This involves normalization of the resolution,
conversion to grayscale, removance of noise and extraction of
regions of interest covering important sections of the
certificate like logos, seals, signatures and blocks of text. This
preprocessing is needed to make it resistant to differences
among certificate templates among institutions and education
levels.

Detection.

A hybrid CNN BiLSTM will be used to identify forged
certificates prior to blockchain registration. The convolutional
neural network element is used to characterize high-level
spatial and visual details on certificate images, including
minute traces left by artifacting methods, like copy-move,
splicing, or resampling. These characteristics are font
inconsistencies, alignment, textures, and embedded graphical
objects.
The feature maps that have been extracted are then
mapped back into sequential form and conjected into
bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) network.
BiLSTM provides contextual and semantic dependencies
among certificate areas and thus the model detects structural
abnormalities that cannot be detected using visual analysis.
The last classification stage gives a binary result as to whether
the certificate is genuine or a counterfeit. Certificates that are
certified as authentic are only sent to blockchain registration
phase, and thus, frauds are not entrenched.
Training Architecture Model and Architecture.
The CNN -BiLSTM network was created in a way that
achieves a compromise between detection performance and
computational cost. The CNN backbone is made up of four
layers of convolutional filters of size 3× 3, ReLU activation
and max-pooling, to obtain hierarchical spatial attributes. The
feature maps were compressed into sequential embeddings
and sent to a layer of BiLSTM with 128 hidden units in each
direction to learn the contextual dependencies between
certificate areas. The Adam optimizer was used to train the
model with an initial learning rate of 1x10-4 which was chosen
empirically to provide a stable convergence. The binary crossentropy loss was used because the problems of forgery
detection are binary. Overfitting was avoided by dropping
(rate = 0.5). The 40 epochs of training were run using early
stopping, which was determined on validation loss. These
hyperparameters are selected based on initial experiments
which showed the best trade-off results between recall and
stability in training.

Off-Chain storage security assumptions and threats model
Although IPFS offers content-addressable, decentralized
storage, there are certain threat vectors that are introduced by
its usage and have to be dealt with. The risks are the
unauthorized access to the content, the denial-of-service
attacks on the IPFS gateways, and the inability of the content
to be available because of the lack of pinning. The framework
proposed helps to eliminate these dangers by storing
encrypted certificate files off-chain, and integrity verification
by on-chain cryptographic hashes. Although an opponent gets
access to content stored in IPFS, it is impossible to alter such
content due to modification attack, as any alteration of the
content modifies its identifier (CID), mismatching with the
hash stored in the blockchain during verification. In order to
reduce availability attacks, the deployment model
presupposes the existence of institutional pinning services and
redundant IPFS nodes. Notably, it is the combination of IPFS
and cryptographic attachments to blockchain that is trusted,
and the off-chain compromise has no chance of being
converted to an on-chain trust violation.

The proposed methodology will secure that only certified
and legitimate certificates are stored permanently since hybrid
forgery detection can be used with decentralized storage and
blockchain-based verification. This integrated AI-blockchain
pipeline can be used to a large extent to boost trust, security
and scale in country-wide educational certificates verification
systems.

E. Certificate Verification Process.
The third party bodies which initiate verification include
employers or educational institutions. Verifiers post the
certificate submitted by a candidate or give the identifier of
the certificate. The system recomputes the hash of the
submitted document and makes a comparison with the hash
that is stored in the blockchain.
When the certificate matches successfully, then the
certificate is authentic and when it does not match it is a sign
of tampering or forgery. Verification queries are nontransactional, thus there are no blockchain transaction charges,
making them cost effective and fast to execute.

without exposing raw certificate data to enhance
generalization without compromising the confidentiality.

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. Experimental Design and Measures of Evaluation.
The experiment on the implementation of the proposed AIenhanced blockchain-organized certificate verification
framework was assessed based on the dataset of authentic and
forged educational certificates that were gathered in various
institutions and certificate templates. The dataset has different
types of forgery that include copy-move manipulation, text
manipulation, logo manipulation, and signature manipulation,
which are realistic attacker situations. Hybrid CNNBiLSTM
model was trained and evaluated on 80:20 train test split and
evaluated with standard classification measures such as
accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and AUC. Besides that,
the system-level performance indicators like verification
latency and blockchain transaction cost were measured to
determine the practical feasibility.

Generalization of Institution Within-Subject Evaluation
In order to measure the generalizability, cross-dataset
validation was done by training the CNN–BiLSTM model on
certificates on a subset of institutions and testing it on
certificates issued by unseen institutions with different
templates and formatting styles. This configuration replicates
real world deployment environment whereby there are
substantial differences in certificate arrangements across
education boards and universities.
The model was found to have an average test accuracy of
96.9 and a recall of 97.4 in cross-institution testing indicating
high institutional drift robustness. The degradation of
performance compared to in-distribution testing was not very
large, which suggests that the hybrid architecture can capture
the invariant structural and semantic characteristics instead of
being selective of template artifacts. This validates the
appropriateness of the proposed system to nationwide or
multi-institution implementations.
B. Detection Performance under Forgery
The CNNBilSTM model proposed has good forgery
detection skills with an accuracy of 98.3, precision of 97.9,
recall of 98.6, and F1-score of 98.2. The fact that the model
has a high recall means that it is very effective at detecting
forged certificates, which is paramount in ensuring that
fraudulent documents do not get anchored to the blockchain.
The hybrid model is invariably more effective than standalone
CNN and Transformer-based baselines, especially in recall
and F1-score. This progress can be made possible through the

performance is effective in enhancing blockchain integrity
without affecting the notion of decentralization.

E. Latency and Analysis of Computational Overhead.
The use of AIs to detect forgery incurs computational
expenses at the certificate issuance stage. Nevertheless, such
overhead is only realized once per certificate and is acceptable
when issuing certificates by institutions. On the contrary,
certificate verification is light, only a hash computations and
blockchain query operations are necessary. Experimental
results show that the verification latency does not increase
significantly relative to the original system of ElimuChain,
which proves that the additional AI layer is not the source of
the worsening of the user experience of verifiers, e.g.
employers or institutions of higher learning.
