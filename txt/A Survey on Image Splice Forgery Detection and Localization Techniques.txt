Abstract—In this research, image splice detection and localization are explored. A spliced image is a digital image that
has been copied and recreated on another image. The cropped
image may undergo resizing, rotation, blurring, etc., before
being forged. With the advancement of many photo-shop tools
and software, image manipulation, and splicing have become a
significant issue of concern. It is being misused nowadays in many
fields like changing will, court orders, official documents, photo
morphing, etc. Excellent forgeries are so deceptive that they go
undetected by the naked eye. Traditional picture tamper detection
algorithms many times fail to detect innovative image splicing.
In the past few years, solutions based on deep learning have been
proposed for tampered image detection and are found to be more
efficient than other techniques. In this research paper, we review
relevant research articles on image splicing, both detection, and
localization, for the period 2003 to 2022.
Index Terms—Image splice, forgery detection, splice localization, splice detection.

There are several methods developed for detecting forgery
in digitally scanned documents. The active and passive approaches are the most common classifications (Fig. 2).
The active approaches mainly focus on data concealment
techniques, such as digital watermarking or copyrighting, in
which previous knowledge is required as critical and necessary
to the process. At the same time, passive methods do not
need any knowledge of the original image. In passive blind
approaches, the detector only has access to the end product.
This approach only uses the given input image to extract
information such as texture, features, color, etc. Information
regarding the original image is not required, hence known as
a blind approach.

Retouching includes enhancing or reducing certain features
of an image by removing all flaws from a photograph, which
commonly entails colour and tone correction, the removal of
blemishes and under-eye circles, deleting parts of an image, as
well as changes to brightness, contrast, and saturation. In Fig.

and saturation has been increased compared to the adjacent
image.

a part of a picture and pastes it onto another image. Here two
image sources are hence required to create the forged image.

B. Image Splice Forgery Detection Techniques
Most traditional splice forgery detection techniques are not
efficient enough to detect image tampering. However, modern
deep learning techniques that use CNN-induced methods are
more efficient and effective [18]. Due to the complexity in
detection spliced images, several researchers came up with
different approaches [6]. Some only detect spliced forgery
regions, while others can detect and localize spliced regions.
Some techniques even detect blurred images after splicing
[19].
One standard method to detect image splice forgery is using
LBP and DCT approach [1]. Here, the local binary pattern
corresponding to an image is initially computed using its
YCbCr chrominance, and from that, it is transformed to its
frequency domain by applying DCT. Then it is sent for further
splice detection. Another standard method referred to was that
of detecting spliced images via camera noise detection [2].
Another technique referred to was that of reliability fusion
maps, which is efficient for localization of even tiny patches
of image pixels [3]. ELA (Error Level Analysis) is yet another
effective method for splice detection [4] [5], which, when
combined with other localization algorithms [23], can provide
more effective outputs. In the recent past, deep convolution
neural network has set the best results in all benchmark
data sets. CNN based papers were the most cited due to it’s
effective feature extraction capability and classification [21].
In the following subsection, we review starting from feature
based techniques to the more recent deep neural network based
methods.
1) Feature Based Techniques: Feature extraction is one of
the early method used in detecting image splicing. Over the
years, many techniques have been proposed for image splice
detection using feature-based techniques. One of the early
features used in image splice detection is the Local Binary

Pattern (LBP) [1], Error Level Analysis (ELA) [4] [5], Discrete
Cosine Transform (DCT), Markov Transform model, etc.
Feature-based techniques are used to enhance the features
of an image; feature extraction becomes more effortless, and
so does the classification. This stage is known as the preprocessing stage. This pre-processing stage is optional in most
cases, as shown in the Fig. 5. However, effective use of this
stage can give better results. Some of them are given in table
II.
In [1], an image splice detection method using LBP along
with DCT is proposed. The LBP code of each block is
found after the chromatic image component is divided into
overlapping blocks, and it is then transformed into its DCT
domain. Later the SVM classifier is being used here for
classification. We can observe that using this technique, the
authors have reached an accuracy of 97.5 on the CASIA V2.0
dataset.
In [4], a technique combining both Error Level Analysis and
Convolution Neural Network is proposed. First, the data set
is divided into forged and original images, after which VGG
16 is used to train because VGG is excellent for training with
small data sets. Although an excellent deep learning model
is used for splice detection, we can observe from the results
that architecture that uses a combination of two or more preprocessing stages is getting better results.
In [5], they have proposed a technique based on ELA
and LBP for detecting spliced images. Instead of one, two
feature extractors are used on the input image, i.e., ELA
and LBP. Then, these features are fused and fed into the
classifier, and these features are classified into authentic and
tampered images. Finally, they accurately locate the tampered
area through the improved ELA method. The authors suggest
at the end that if this combination of ELA and LBP is
implemented on CNN, there are higher chances of getting even
better accuracy.

3) Forgeries based on inconsistency in noise levels of
image: Image splicing can also be detected using the noise
inconsistencies within the input image. This technique is based
on the variation in noise consistencies of different images
depending upon the place, time, and model with which it
has been taken. The table shows that detection accuracy has
increased over the years with the efficient utilization of noise
in images for detection of forgery.
In [9], the image is split into non-overlapping blocks, which
are then clustered to create fresh and modified chunks. To get
at the final result, the found suspicious zones are subdivided
again to refine noise estimation and then classified. The author
segmented the image into sections with considerably different
noise variances to enhance the results even further to estimate
local noise variances. A simple k-means clustering technique
enhanced the outcome, followed by post-processing processes
on the foundation areas. In [10] a method that is automated
Spliced forgery detection in raw photos has been suggested.
They looked at the relative consistency of noise parameters. To
identify the spliced pictures, image discrepancies from quadtree decomposition. In [8], a method based on the premise that
the level of noise in the original and the image to be spliced
varies. The picture is first divided into superpixels and then
extracted into ordinary forms. Three color channel differences
are recovered from each superpixel, and the residual noise
level is evaluated. Finally, the superpixels are classified as
tampered or original by clustering them into two classes using
the Farthest Distributed Centroids Clustering algorithm.

5) Illumination color Estimation: Detecting image splicing
using illumination color estimation is yet another technique
commonly used. This technique is implemented by using
the physical environment of the image taken. Image splicing
can be easily identified using Illumination color Estimation
[18], where there will be changes in the illumination of the
background, say sunset or sunrise, and color changes, if any.
Hence if the images are taken from the same lighting condition
and colors, the detection accuracy may reduce. In this type of

method, too, accuracy increases as color detection efficiency
increases, such as pixel and edge-based illuminance color
estimation. In [13] we can see that the chrominance and luminance of the input image is determined, and illumination is
calculated from luminance using the Illumination-Reflectance
model. Finally, the feature vector is found using LBP for
classification.
In [18], we can find a new approach was induced for
detecting photographic splicing that combines the excellent
representation capability of Illuminant Maps and convolution
neural networks as a way of learning the essential signals
of a counterfeit straight from accessible training data. The
authors have obtained an accuracy of 96 for the same. In [12]
the spliced pictures are discovered on image areas utilising
pixel and edge-based illuminant colour estimation. The shape
and colour characteristics are retrieved from these illuminant
estimators, then fed into SVM for classification.

V. CONCLUSION
Although there were several techniques implemented for the
successful detection of image splicing, there was a lacking
of an effective review paper stating and comparing the best
techniques in use. Hence this paper was bought up to fill up
that gap in the research field of image splice detection as well
as localization. In this paper some of the important research
works in image splice detection during the period of 2003 to
2022 were reviewed. Several techniques by which image splice
detection as well as localization are found were also discussed
and compared. After going through several literature reviews,
it was noted that there are several traditional methods, such as
watermarking, camera response, illuminance based techniques,
lighting based methods, RGB/YCbCr color component, etc.,
used in the detection of spliced images. However, we can
observe that techniques that use CNN within it surpasses all
other techniques in terms of cost and accuracy. So a CNNinduced method, when used successfully by imposing onto
other traditional algorithms, can result in an excellent image
splice detection and localization, which shall be of great help
in the future.
