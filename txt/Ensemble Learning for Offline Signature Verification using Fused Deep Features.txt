Abstract— Signature verification plays an important role in
document authentication. The efficient and robust system is
required for forgery detection in documents in the presence of
distortion such as rotation, scaling and noise. This paper
proposes a writer-dependent signature verification method,
where hand-crafted features are fused with one-dimensional
convolutional neural network features to cater different
variance in the whole verification process. Proposed feature
extraction methodology is validated by bagged ensemble
learning technique in which machine learning classifier’s
decision is finalized based on majority voting. The effectiveness
of the proposed signature verification approach has been
evaluated across three distinct publicly accessible datasets. The
outcomes highlight the versatility of this method. Moreover, the
proposed system surpasses the performance of other preexisting systems documented in the literature.
Keywords — signature verification, 1-d deep features, handcrafted features, bagged ensemble learning, machine learning

I. INTRODUCTION
Biometrics is employed in many different security
applications, and nowadays has gained more research interest
among researchers across the globe. Biometrics can be
categorized as physiological or behavioural properties.
Physiological applications can be the face, iris, palm,
fingerprint, and behavioral applications, such as handwriting,
gait, voice, and signatures. A signature is a handwritten
sample of a person’s handwriting style that specifies their
name, nickname, last name, or a mixture of the words used for
identification, and authenticating these signatures is called
signature verification. Signature verification is a crucial task
in various domains, ranging from financial institutions to legal
documentation and beyond. Traditionally, human experts
have been responsible for determining the authenticity of
signatures, a process that is time-consuming, subjective, and
susceptible to errors. Signature verification processes are
primarily of two types: offline and online [1].
Input images to verify offline signatures are often captured
using a mobile or scanned one. To verify the online signature,
a person usually uses a stylus to input their signature, which is
recorded with a timestamp and signature position. In contrast
to this, offline process can be catagorized either writerindependent or writer-dependent. Every time a new user's
signature is added to the system, the system must be updated
and retrained in a writer-dependent scenario. This makes the
procedure burdensome and impractical. In an authorindependent scenario, however, a generalized system that can
distinguish between authentic and forged signatures without
repetitive retraining must be developed. However, the
introduction of machine learning techniques has brought

about a substantial transformation in the field of signature
verification, offering a more efficient, accurate, and
automated approach.
Deep learning, a subset of machine learning, has proven to
be highly effective in automatically learning and representing
intricate patterns within data. When applied to signature
verification, deep learning models can extract discriminative
features that capture the unique characteristics of each
individual's signature. This technology holds the promise of
streamlining processes, reducing the risk of fraud, and
increasing the overall efficiency of document authentication.
In this paper, we are presenting the fusion of hand-crafted
features with the 1-d deep features with ensemble learning.
Ensemble learning implies the combination of multiple
individual models to design an accurate, predictive, and
stronger prototype. When applied to signature verification
using 1-d deep learning and hand-crafted features, ensemble
technique significantly enhances the robustness and reliability
of the authentication process. This approach leverages the
diversity of multiple models to improve generalization,
mitigate overfitting, and ultimately increase the overall
accuracy of signature authentication.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW

effectively executed to discern the authenticity of a signature
image from whether it's been forged or not. However, an
approach called dynamic time warping, which evaluates the
alignment between two evolving structures, has been
employed to differentiate between authentic and forged
samples [3]. Furthermore, a feature extractor based on the
local random transform has emerged [4]. Additionally, novel
methods for verifying individual signatures have been

Despite these techniques, most of the contemporary
learning relies on feature learning representations using deep
neural networks (DNNs) to authenticate signatures. In
contrast to employing manually crafted features, Dey et al.
introduced the SigNet network model for independent offline
signature validation, utilizing a Siamese network based on
CNN. Their approach involves assessing the similarities
between pairs of observations, decreasing the Euclidean
distance for similar pairs, and increasing the distance for
dissimilar pairs [11]. Another group designed a CNN
architecture with 18 layers, consisting of convolutional,
pooling, rectified linear unit (ReLU), normalization, and fully
connected layers. The output of one layer serves as input for
the next, creating high-dimensional features and enhancing
accuracy [12]. DNNs have been favored for offline signature
verification due to their ability to self-learn for handwritten
signature authentication.
Hafemann et al. suggested employing a deep CNN to
extract features solely from input images, and this model was
trained using a distinct image group [13, 14]. A Siamese
network based on CNN was proposed for offline signature
verification [15]. Similarly, another CNN-based solution for
both writer-dependent and writer-independent signature
verification was introduced the same year [16]. Transfer
learning (TL) models based on GoogleNet inception V1 and
V3 were also utilized on the Grupo de Procesado Digital de
Seales (GPDS) dataset in the same year [17]. In 2020,
Siamese networks were applied to three distinct datasets GPDS, MCYT 75, and BHsig260 - alongside statistical
measures to enhance model performance [18]. However,
CNNs require minimal preprocessing and are a variant of the
multilayer perceptron. In modern machine learning tasks,
these computational models based on biological neurons
surpass earlier forms of artificial intelligence (AI). LargeScale Visual Recognition Challenge (LSVRC) exemplifies a
large-scale task, with DL CNN methods exhibiting the
highest accuracy in the ImageNet challenge [19]. Recent
research on the ImageNet dataset has showcased state-of-theart accuracy results [20].
III. PROPOSED METHOLODOLOGY
In our paper, the fusion of hand-crafted and 1-D CNN
features is presented. The proposed methodology is given in
Fig. 1. Initially, the signature dataset has been divided into
two parts i.e., training and testing data. The split we consider
for the training and testing is 70:30 ratio. The fundamental
pre-processing such as identifying the signatures on the
document and image resizing has been done on the
experimental dataset. After extracting the features from deep
models which includes VGG16, DenseNet, MobileNet as
well as hand-crafted features with 1-D CNN, it is passed to
the machine learning classifier and the bagged ensemble
learning technique is applied.

A. Hand-Crafted Features
For signature authentication in digital documents, the issue
of affine transformations such as rotation and scale variance
are very important. The hand-crafted features are there to
cater for the issues of rotation and scale invariance. These
hand-crafted features are classified into structural-based,
statistical-based, and model-based approaches. We are using
widely used rotation invariant Local Binary Pattern (LBP),
Gabor, and gray-level co-occurrence features from document
textures which helps in distinguishing the different
signatures. These features are concatenated for each signature
sample and then forwarded to 1-D CNN architecture. Ojala et
al. [21] demonstrated Local Binary Pattern (LBP) features
which aim to compare neighboring grayscale image pixels.
LBP has high discriminative power and gives better results in
less computational time. Due to its simple implementation, it
can be used for signature discrimination conveniently. Its
equation is given as

B. 1-D Convolutional Neural Network
After extracting the hand-crafted features of signatures,
it will pass on the one-dimensional deep learning model. The
reason of using 1-D CNN model is to capture local patterns
and features within sequences. Convolutional layers slide a
kernel over the input data, identifying relevant patterns in
small segments. This enables the model to recognize
distinctive features such as low-level and high-level features
regardless of their position in the sequence. Similarly, by
combining multiple convolutional layers, 1D CNNs can
capture compositional features that arise from the
combination of simpler patterns. 1D CNNs inherently
possess a scale of translation variety, allowing them to
identify samples regardless of their precise location in the
input sequence. This property is particularly valuable in the
signature where the position of feature can vary.
The detail of the layers of 1-D CNN model is given in Fig.
2. The proposed CNN model is less complex and less
computationally intense. The proposed model only has six
layers including a flatten, ReLU, batch normalization, dropout
and dense, and one convolutional layer.

C. VGG16
VGG16, a CNN architecture designed by the Visual
Geometry Group (VGG), was declared winner of 2014
ILSVRC object identification algorithm [23]. Main impact of
VGG model was to confirm that the expansion of network
depth can boost the performance of model. VGG-16 develops
on the guidelines of AlexNet by utilizing several convolution

cores, which widen the intensity of the architecture and
reduced total parameters [24].
D. MobileNet
MobileNet [25] is distinguished from other extant
models by the reduced number of parameters compared to
others. It is composed of depthwise discrete convolutions,
which were devised to shrink the computing attempts in the
first few levels. Its two layers are depthwise and pointwise
convolutions. depthwise convolution refers to the process of
filtering the input without adding new features. As an
outcome, the technique that produces novel descriptors,
pointwise convolution, was incorporated. Consequently, the
term depthwise discrete convolution was introduced to
characterize the combination of two consecutive layers. It
allocates a exclusive filter to every input using convolutions
and then constructs a dual allocation of output using 1 × 1
conv (pointwise). After each convolution, Batch
Normalization (BN) and Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) are
applied. We extracted the features from fully-connected
layer.
E. Dense Net
Huang et al. introduced the Dense Convolution Neural
Network DenseNet. [26] Each layer in DenseNet induces all
layers in a feed forward approach. Five ranks comprise a
DenseNet unit. The initial 4 layers are dense, followed by a
transition layer. Each stratum of this dense unit has a growing
rate of (k). In the transition layer, 2 × 2 average pooling
layer with a 1 × 1 convolution and a 2 stride is attached to
increase the growth rate to 4. In the dense stratum, there are
1 × 1 and 3 × 3 convolutions having a stride of 1.
DenseNet169 is utilized in our investigation. We extracted
the features from fully connected layer.
F. Bagged Ensamble learning
Ensemble learning describes a broad, effective method to
Machine Learning, that shoots to improve analytical
capacities by merging the forecasts from numerous
simulations. While there are several ensembles to verify
signatures, boosting, stacking, and bagging are the three
approaches that direct the ensemble learning approach. In this
paper, we are using bagging technique. Bagging leads to
averaging the verification of signatures from several models
that have corresponded to different models of the original
datasets.
The signature verification through the ensemble
members is merged by majority polling. Three classifiers
have been used in our experimentation which includes
Support Vector Machine (SVM), Multi-Layer Perceptron
(MLP) and Extreme Gradient Bossing (XGB). The entire
process varies depending on how all dataset models are made
to train ensemble members. The dataset is split up into
distinct sections for each member. These members are chosen
at random.

Three human signature databases, i.e., BHSig260, CEDAR
[27] and SigComp2011[28], were used to test the
performance of the proposed signature verification model.
The BHSig260 dataset has 6,240 real signatures and 7,800 ski
fake signatures. These signatures were collected from 260
people. The CEDAR dataset [27] comprises of 55 person’s
signatures. Each person has presented 24 legitimate signature
sample and 24 forged samples. The dataset contains 1,320 (55
× 24) real signatures and 1,320 forged signatures.
SigComp2011 was published at the ICDAR 2011 conference
for the International Signature Verification Competition
(SVC). [28] The dataset contains of both Chinese and Dutch
authors. The performance measures to validate the proposed
model are Accuracy (ACC), False Acceptance Rate (FAR),
and False Rejection Rate (FRR) [29]. These experiments are
done using affine transformations. In the testing scenarios,
the images are rotated to 90 angle and 10% scaled, as shown
in Fig. 3.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper presented a feature fusion approach in which
hand-crafted and deep CNN features have been combined for
signature verification. The extracted features from VGG16,
MobileNet, DenseNet and 1-D CNN have been compared
with the proposed feature fusion technique and from results
it reveals that it outperforms as compared to the other
techniques. The proposed model has been tested using three
popular machine learning classifiers i.e., SVM, MLP and
XGB. The result of machine learning classifiers has been
selected from bagged ensemble learning approach in which
majority voting selected. Image transformations such as
rotation and scaling have been considered for testing the
feasibility of proposed methodology. Through different
experimentation, it has been revealed that the proposed
methodology outperforms as compared to the existing work
if signature has various transformations other than original
sample. This technique can be further tested for on-line
signatures to analysis the effectiveness of this technique
