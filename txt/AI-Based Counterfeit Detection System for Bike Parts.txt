Abstract—Counterfeiting in the motorcycle industry creates
major threats to safety and ﬁnancial instability for motorcyclists.
For independent mechanics and local consumers, it is very difﬁcult to distinguish between authentic and counterfeit motorbike
parts without assistance from a professional. In this paper we
describe a framework for a veriﬁcation system based on artiﬁcial
intelligence (AI) using Qwen-3-4B-VL Vision-Language Model
(VLM). To develop our veriﬁcation system’s ability to identify
counterfeit motorcycle parts accurately, we built a specialized
dataset using 1,656 high resolution images of important parts
of safety equipment (like helmets) and also engine parts (like
spark plugs and air ﬁlters). Also to keep these images reliable,
we created a ’VLM-in-the-loop’ curation pipeline using LLaMA4-Scout-17B for structure labeling and to validate each image
source. After the completion of indexed training on a dual
NVIDIA Tesla T4 conﬁguration, VLM achieved a maximum of
92.3% accuracy and provided both an interpretive reasoning
document of how it arrived at each conclusion which traditional
Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) do not provide. Lastly,
we illustrate how reliable authentic/counterfeit detection from
a consumer-grade computer running an NVIDIA RTX 3050
can be accomplished at an unprocessed latency of 1.42 seconds,
providing mass fraud detection capabilities to retail consumers.
Index Terms—Counterfeit Detection, Bike Spare Parts, VisionLanguage Model, Deep Learning, Image Classiﬁcation, Artiﬁcial
Intelligence, Web Deployment.

The advancement of Artiﬁcial Intelligence (AI) technology,
especially in the areas of computer vision and deep learning
[6], now allows for the creation of automated image-based
veriﬁcation systems to detect counterfeit motorcycle parts. For
example, vision-based models can identify subtle changes in
the visual appearance of parts that may be very difﬁcult for a
human to detect. Additionally, in more recent design iterations
of Vision-Language models (VLMs), it is now possible to
classify images using both visual and descriptive information
from the same image and achieve a higher classiﬁcation
accuracy than is possible by using either method alone [5],
[10]. Therefore, combining visual features of an image with
contextual information derived from the visual features will
improve the accuracy of VLM-based classiﬁers.
This research proposes the development of an Al-based
counterfeit detection system for motorcycle spare parts using
a VLM-based image classiﬁcation model. For training, we
collected a structured dataset that consists of genuine and
counterfeit bike component images. The system provides
image preprocessing, feature extraction, image classiﬁcation
using deep learning, and outputs the prediction results in a
web interface. The primary goal of this system is to develop
an automated, scalable, accurate, and dependable solution for
authenticating counterfeit bike parts in real-time. By providing
a solution for manufacturers, service centers, dealers, and
consumers, this project will help reduce the prevalence of
counterfeit spare parts in the marketplace.

I. I NTRODUCTION
The Motorcycle Spare Parts market is developing rapidly as
a result of an increase in demand for spare parts and replacement parts. As a side effect of this rapid growth, counterfeit
spare parts for motorcycles are now widely available in the
marketplace. Counterfeit Spare Parts are identical or substantially identical to the originals but are manufactured without
the authorization of the original company; such parts look like
original parts but have no real quality, durability, or safety
standards. Therefore, using such counterfeit parts can lead to
serious mechanical failure, diminishing vehicle performance,
reduction in proﬁt and, ultimately, road accidents.
Traditional methods to identify counterfeit motorcycle spare
parts rely heavily on an inspection performed manually by a
trained mechanic or an authorized dealer. Inspection methods include examining the quality of packaging, holographic
labels, serial numbers, the weight of the parts, as well as
minor design discrepancies. However, due to the continuous

II. L ITERATURE S URVEY
Counterfeit product detection is an important area of study
and has gained signiﬁcant momentum as the world continues
to experience a surge of fake products/brands throughout
multiple industry sectors. Researchers have taken a variety of
machine learning and deep learning approaches to detecting
counterfeit products by examining the visual appearance of
both the product and its packaging and the serial number
associated with each product.

E. Research Gap
Most existing systems primarily focus on general consumer
goods or high-end products. A limited number of research
studies speciﬁcally target counterfeit detection within the twowheeler segment of the automotive industry. Furthermore,
most existing systems are based on either traditional image
processing methods or CNN-based classiﬁcation [7], [9],
without taking into account the importance of contextual
understanding of images. Hence, a new advanced solution is
required that combines visual intelligence with multimodality.

A. Traditional Image Processing Techniques
Early counterfeit detection systems utilized traditional image processing methods including edge detection, texture analysis, and color histogram comparisons. Such methods include
(but are not limited to) the Histogram of Oriented Gradients
(HOG), Local Binary Patterns (LBP), and the Scale-Invariant
Feature Transform (SIFT). These features are subsequently
used to identify the authenticity of the products using various
machine learning algorithms such as Support Vector Machines
(SVM) and k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN) [6].

III. R ESEARCH M ETHODOLOGY AND DATA S OURCE
A. Data Curation and Labeling using Algorithms
The availability of standardized, expert-veriﬁed datasets has
proven to be a signiﬁcant barrier to the detection of counterfeit
products. To overcome this limitation, we created a custom
dataset of 1,656 high-resolution images of three different
categories of products that have industrial signiﬁcance; these
are helmets, spark plugs, and air ﬁlters. These products are
very important for assessing the safety and reliability of
products. We used the Groq API and LLaMA-4-Scout-17B
[12] in a VLM-in-the-loop architecture to create an automatic
quality control pipeline to eliminate the potential for human
errors associated with manual labeling and improve the overall
reliability of the dataset. This will help provide the model with
additional reference data through the incorporation of domain
knowledge, which may include verifying whether a helmet
meets ISI/DOT safety standards or whether the electrode
spacing of a spark plug is symmetrical. Regularizing the
datasets provided high-precision ground truth labels.

B. Machine Learning-Based Classiﬁcation
As supervised machine learning advanced, researchers
started to use traditional classiﬁers from the machine learning
suite such as Logistic Regression, Random Forests, and Naı̈ve
Bayes. Although these types of machine learning classiﬁers
increased the accuracy of classiﬁcation compared to simple
rules-based systems, they heavily relied on feature engineering. Classiﬁcation performance signiﬁcantly dropped when
processing products with complex design models or products
with subtle differences from genuine goods.
C. Image-Based Counterfeit Detection using Deep Learning
The development of Convolutional Neural Networks
(CNNs) have made a signiﬁcant impact on image classiﬁcation
tasks [1]. State-of-the-art deep learning models including VGGNet, ResNet [2], and MobileNet have demonstrated outstanding capacity to learn the hierarchical visual features of raw
image data automatically. To date, a number of studies have
used CNN architectures for product authentication and fakeproduct classiﬁcation. These CNN models have signiﬁcantly
reduced the need for manual feature extraction while improving accuracy in real-world applications. However, CNN-based
solutions are primarily focused on visual features and do not
include contextual or descriptive data about the products being
classiﬁed.

IV. P ROPOSED S YSTEM
In order to resolve the downsides of manual inspection,
as well as the limitations of conventional CNN-based classiﬁcation systems, we have created a modular AI framework
utilizing the Qwen-3-4B-VL Vision-Language Model (VLM)
[11]. This design bridges the performance gap between highcomputation training environments and resource-limited edge
deployment locations.
A. System Description
Our framework implements a complete pipeline system containing a user-facing interface, an automation layer for image
preprocessing, a reasoning engine based on VLM technology,
and a ﬁnal classiﬁcation function. Our framework differs
from conventional classiﬁcation pipelines because, in addition
to producing binary outputs, our system assesses structural
details and provides reasoning for each determination made.
The logical ﬂow and high-level interaction between these
components are illustrated in the overall system architecture
shown in Fig. 1.

context in which the image is represented [5]. VLMs leverage the relationship between visual features and descriptive
properties of objects in order to improve the robustness of
classiﬁcation. VLMs are high-performing for creating captions, answering questions through visual questions [10], and

V. M ODULES D ESCRIPTION
The proposed AI counterfeit detection system has been
organized into several specialized functional modules, each
module operating on a key stage of the authenticating pipeline:
• The Image Input Module is the ﬁrst (and therefore,
primary) system entry point and allows users to upload
images of components in standard formats (i.e., JPG,
JPEG, or jpeg).
• The Dataset Management Module will allow for organization of the 1,656 images created from the “VLM-inthe-loop” pipeline, and allow for management of the highﬁdelity labels generated from LLaMA-4-Scout-17B [12]
regarding structural integrity and safety certiﬁcations.
• The Image Preprocessing Module prepares the raw
uploads to assist with ensuring there is model consistency
(i.e., dimensional adjustments, pixel value normalization,
and noise suppression ﬁlters).
• The Vision-Language Feature Extraction Module extracts deep semantic and visual features by utilizing
the Qwen-3-4B-VL architecture [11], and is speciﬁcally
tuned for identifying subtle “red ﬂags,” such as typeface
inconsistencies on decals, and subtle electrode misalignments.
• The Classiﬁcation Module is the last module to process
the extracted feature embeddings and to generate the predicted authenticity class and conﬁdence score associated
with that prediction.

A. Model Training Parameters
The model was trained using a supervised learning method.
The Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 2e-4 was utilized
for training. Due to the complexity of the VLM and because
of the speciﬁc hardware limitations we had, we set a batch size
of 16 for training so that we could maintain a stable gradient
descent while allowing the VLM to capture visually detailed
images.

C. Infrastructure and Hyperparameters
Our training utilized a dual NVIDIA Tesla T4 conﬁguration
on Kaggle, providing a total of 32 GB of VRAM. We
applied Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA) [13] and
limited training to four epochs, as validation loss stabilized
signiﬁcantly after the second epoch. We utilized a small batch
size with 8 gradient accumulation steps to ensure the model
focused on subtle visual “red ﬂags” essential in identifying
counterfeit products.
D. Edge-Optimized Deployment
The model was optimized for deployment via 4-bit GGUF
quantization [14], reducing the memory footprint to 2.8GB.
This hardware conﬁguration maintains a fast inference time
of 1.42 seconds on an NVIDIA RTX 3050, enabling highreasoning counterfeit detection at the edge.
