Abstract— A common biometric for establishing a person's
identity in document forensics is verification of handwritten
signatures. Signature plays an important role in banking,
financial, commercial and so on. However, issues associated
with signatures are numerous since any two signatures may
look very similar with little to no differences written by the
same person. Despite the enormous research efforts, offline
signature verification is still difficult, especially when
attempting to differentiate between competent forgeries and
authentic signatures because the visual differences between the
two can sometimes be less noticeable than between two
authentic ones. Due to the expanding usage of digital signatures
and electronic documents, there has been an increase in the
demand for effective and precise signature forgery detection
systems (SFDS). In order to effectively combat handwritten
signature forgery, this study intends to create an automated
system utilizing a deep neural network (DNN) especially a
simple and efficient Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) that
can determine if a given signature is real or faked. The model
will take signature image as input and extract important
features through different layers of CNN that will helpful in
distinguishing genuine and forged signatures. The model
performance is also accessed with different image resizing
techniques and optimizers used in training of CNN. The
model's performance in terms of a set of assessment metrics,
such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score are provided
in this paper after extensive tests are conducted across a huge
dataset of handwritten signatures including both genuine and
various types of forged signatures. Additionally, the model's
performance is also contrasted with a cutting-edge deep
learning model. The simulation results conclusively
demonstrate the appropriateness of the suggested methodology
for automatically detecting forgery in handwritten signature
images.

I. INTRODUCTION

special and consistent manner. It is a unique and personal
expression of one’s identity. A common biometric for
establishing a person's identity in document forensics is
verification of handwritten signatures. Signature serves as a
form of biometric authentication or verification of the
person’s identity or consent on various kinds of documents
that require such confirmation. For example, a signature can
be used to authorize a payment on a bank cheque, to agree to
the terms and conditions of a contract, to indicate the sender
or recipient of a letter, or to provide or request information
on a form. However, issues associated with signatures are

In [4] the authors have proposed the use of a Siamese
Neural Networks to assist in resolving the offline
handwritten signature verification problem in a writerindependent context. They include the image binarization in
the pre-processing step to convert the original image to a
single channel grey scale image. Then Morphological
erosion is used to increase the signature strokes, with an
image resizing of 128 x 128 pixels. Augmentation and
synthetic signature generation is also performed.
Performance of the model is then compared with other pretrained networks such as LeNet and SigNet with accuracy
and error rate metrics.
A simple CNN with three convolution and maxpooling
layer arranged in alternate fashion with one flatten and dense
layer is proposed in [5] for signature forgery detection. The
model is tested on a dataset including 6000 images prepared
by the author with one person making originals and two
others making the forgeries. As all images are in RGB
format, so they are converted to grey scale image in preprocessing stage. The greyscale images of the handwritten
signatures are then converted to Bitmap format to rasterize
the signature dataset. Accuracy and loss percentage metrics
is used for model assessment.
In [6] another CNN model derived from the per-trained
ResNet-50 as its base model, and adding additional layers on
top is proposed as a SFDS. The model has used various
autoencoders for feature extraction. The model is tested with
a dataset including 2660 english signatures collected from
kaggle dataset.
In [7] authors have used a CNN model for detecting
signature forgery. The CNN model was trained and tested
for various different dataset split ratios. Loss and accuracy
was used to judge the performance of the model.
In [8] the authors have used attendance sheets to
generate handwritten signature dataset. OMR (Optical Mark
Recognition) tools were used to scan the attendance sheets,
the OMR tools perform operations like correcting the
orientation and identify its class by its unique barcode
present in the attendance sheet. To distinguish between
signatures and non-signature, a feed forward neural network
was utilized. A deep CNN classifier was then proposed to
check the legitimacy of the signatures. Various hyperparameter tuning was also performed to increase the
accuracy of the model. Optimizer like ADAM and SGD
were also compared. A confusion matrix was used to
evaluate the performance of the neural network model.
A comparison of a custom CNN, VGG16, a Siamese
Neural Network(SNN) and a SNN with SigNet architecture
is presented in [9] for signature forgery detection. By testing
the model on CEDAR dataset, the custom SNN shows better
accuracy compared to the other models.
A 14 layer CNN using 6 convolutions, 5 maxpooling, 1
flatten and 2 dense layers is proposed in [10] to verify
authenticity of handwritten signatures. The model is tested
on a dataset including 300 images with equal proportion of
real and forged ones related to signatures of 30 people. The
images are re-scaled to a standard image size of 512 x 512
pixels. The model performance is also accesses with three
different split ratios, out of which the 80:20 ratio has shown
the best accuracy in training and validation set.

II. PROPOSED CNN BASED SFDS
Signature forgery is a serious problem that can cause
financial and legal damages to individuals and
organizations. In this study, we propose a simple custom
CNN model for signature forgery detection that can
automatically learn discriminative features from the
signature images and classify them as genuine or forged one,
assuming it as a binary classification problem.
Fig. 1 depicts the layout of the developed 7-layer CNN
based SFDS model including 3 convolution layers with
associated maxpooling and batch normalization, one dropout
layer, one flatten layer and two fully connected dense layers.
The several convolutional, pooling, and fully connected
layers included in the network helps to capture the subtle
variations and characteristics of different signatures. The
input layer, the convolution layer, the pooling layer, the
flatten layer, the fully linked dense layer and the output layer
are the six fundamental layers that make up the network.
The signature images passing through the input layer are
converted to matrix of pixels. The input layer’s size is 227 x
227 x 3 pixels. In the first convolution layer, 32filters with
kernel size of 3x3 has been used. The second convolution
layers consist of 64 filters with 3x3 as the kernel size. The
third convolution layers uses 128 filters with the same kernel
size as the previous layer. All the three convolutional layers
use rectified linear (ReLu) as the activation function. The
first two convolution layers use MaxPooling while the third
convolution layer uses GlobalAveragePooling followed by a
fully connected dense layer with sigmoid activation
function. Batch normalization is also performed after the

used to plot performance graphs for training and validation
accuracy and loss. The SK.learn library provided us with

IV. CONCLUSION
In this study, performance of three DNN based SFDS
models such as the proposed simple 7 layer CNN, pre-trained
VGG-16, trainable VGG-16 are observed for detecting forged
and genuine cases from signature images. By training the
model over the popular CEDAR dataset and analysing its
results over validation and test set, it is evident that the
suggested CNN based SFDS model having a simple
architecture with fewer layers able to detect the forgery
images from the image pool with a greater accuracy compared
to the other two models. Further observing the model
performance with three optimizers and three split ratios, it is
clear that the SGD optimizer with split ration of 80:20 is best
suitable for the proposed CNN model yielding the best
training, validation and testing accuracy. Despite the
remarkable accuracy generated by the proposed model, further
improvements can be made by using data augmentation to
generate a huge synthetic dataset to enhance the training of the
model. In future, different hyper parameters can be tuned and
adjusted by optimization techniques for further refinement of
the CNN model.
